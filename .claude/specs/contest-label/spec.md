# Contest label

Vet: 7/10 full-build 2026-10-12 pair: .claude/pairs/contest-label sha: e860de0a

## Summary

During the draft a rival's road can point at the spot a settlement planned to expand to, and the rival then settles it first. Best picks prices that spot as if nobody else wanted it and says nothing. This spec marks, on the board and on the row, each spot a pick's setup road leads to that a rival's road already ends on or ends one road short of. No score, ranking or road changes.

Best picks has been computed by the Rust engine inside `src/engine/advice/engine.wasm` since 2026-10-12: commits `2f61a9e0` and `fbef1ce8` deleted the TypeScript analysis and formula (`src/engine/analyze.ts`, `expansion.ts`, `valuation.ts`). The label is therefore derived in TypeScript from two things that already exist: the `firstPick` and `firstRoad` the engine returns for each row, and the pieces on the board. The engine, its response and `engine.wasm` do not change.

A rival two roads away is not marked: on random four-seat boards 54% of settlements point their road at a spot some rival is one or two roads from, against 28% for one road alone (300 boards per seat count, greedy draft, measured on 2026-10-12 with the TypeScript scorer before it was deleted that day; output in `.claude/pairs/cut-off-programme/contest-measure-results.txt`).

Whether the ranking prices contests is a separate, measured question in [`cut-off-programme`](../simulator/cut-off-programme.md). That programme's classifier belongs in the Rust scorer. It shares the rule in requirements 1 and 2 with this spec and none of its code.

## Deferred decisions

Neither choice below blocks this spec. Each is the owner's to make later, and the spec is built as written until the owner says otherwise.

- The engine reports the sites its price sums. The owner decided "app only; no Rust, simulator or wasm" when the engine was TypeScript. The engine's expansion term walks outward from a pick, prices every corner it could later settle, and adds the best two to the pick's score. That walk now exists in Rust only, so those two corners cannot be read in the app without a new field in the engine's response. This spec keeps the decision as written and marks the corners the row's road leads to (requirement 3). If the owner later picks the engine field (first entry under Alternatives considered), requirement 3 and `expansionSpots` are replaced by the field, and requirements 1, 2 and 6 to 12 stand.
- The label reads the likely-gone line. This spec reads placed pieces only (requirement 6), because the board does not draw the line's pieces while a row is hovered or selected, so a ring caused by a predicted road would point at nothing on the board. Reading the line means a row's marks must also draw the predicted pieces that cause a ring (second entry under Alternatives considered).

## Requirements

Terms used below. The board is the `Board` a ranking was computed for: its `buildings` and `roads` are the pieces actually placed. Corners and edges are those of `boardGrid(board.layout)`. `B(v)` is the owner of the building on corner `v` and `E(e)` the owner of the road on edge `e`, either possibly none. The viewed seat is the player the rows are ranked for (`viewedPlayerId` in `src/ui/draftSlots.ts`). A rival is any other player in `board.players`. The two trees are the desktop `Workspace` and the phone's `PhoneShell`, which share `AnalysisPanel` and `BoardCanvas`. A listed row is one of the first `LISTED_PICKS` (5, `src/ui/restMarks.ts`) recommendations, which is all the panel shows. The likely-gone line is the engine's prediction of the picks other seats make before the viewed one (`takenBeforeFirstPick` in the response), shown above the rows; its `Play these out` button places those predicted settlements and roads on the board, and a selected row's `Place settlement` button places them plus that row's pick.

1. A road end of seat `R` is a corner with no building that has an incident edge owned by `R`.
2. Seat `R` contests corner `s` when `s` is a road end of `R`, or `s` has a neighbour `v` across an edge owned by nobody or by `R` and `v` is a road end of `R`. The viewed seat never contests.
3. A row's expansion spots are found from its `firstPick` and `firstRoad`. There are none when `firstRoad` is null, when `firstPick` is not an end of `firstRoad`, or when `E(firstRoad)` is a rival. Otherwise let `n` be the other end of `firstRoad`. A spot is a corner `s` joined to `n` by an edge owned by nobody or by the viewed seat, where no building stands on `s` or on a neighbour of `s`, and `s` is neither `firstPick` nor a neighbour of it. Spots are listed in ascending id of the edge that joins them to `n`. A row has zero, one or two spots.
4. `src/engine/contest.ts` is a new module with no React and no engine call. It exports `expansionSpots(board: Board, playerId: string, firstPick: VertexId, firstRoad: EdgeId | null): VertexId[]` (requirement 3, `playerId` being the viewed seat), `contestRivals(board: Board, playerId: string, site: VertexId): string[]` (the rivals that contest `site` under requirement 2, in `board.players` order) and `rowContest(board: Board, playerId: string, row: Pick<Recommendation, 'firstPick' | 'firstRoad'>): RowContest`, where `interface RowContest { spots: VertexId[]; contested: { vertexId: VertexId; playerId: string }[] }` holds the row's spots and one `contested` entry per contesting rival per spot, ordered by spot and then by roster. It reuses `blockedVertices` from `src/engine/legality.ts` for the building test and `vertexIncidentEdgeIds` and `edgeEndpointVertexIds` from `src/model/coords.ts` for neighbours, keeping only edges in the grid.
5. `src/engine/analysisProtocol.ts` does not change. `Recommendation` mirrors the engine's JSON, so the contest is derived beside it, never stored in it.
6. The label reads the pieces placed on the board and nothing else. The likely-gone line's settlements and roads are not read, because the board does not draw them while a row is hovered or selected (`AnalysisPanel` puts only that row's marks in the store's `highlight`). Every ring and every note therefore has its cause in a rival road drawn on the board. A predicted pick starts to count once it is placed, by hand or by `Play these out`.
7. `AnalysisPanel` (`src/ui/AnalysisPanel.tsx`) computes one `RowContest` per listed row in a single `useMemo`, keyed by the ranking shown, its board and its viewed seat. With no viewed seat nothing is computed. A current ranking uses the store's board and `viewedPlayerId`. While a ranking is pending and the last one is still shown (`stale` in `AnalysisPanel`), the contest is computed from the board that last ranking was computed for and that board's `mePlayerId`, so a held row keeps the note it had and the panel keeps its height. `AnalysisProvider` (`src/ui/AnalysisProvider.tsx`) ranks a copy of the store's board whose `mePlayerId` is set to the viewed seat (`setMe(board, playerId)`), and keeps the last finished ranking with that copy in its `completed` state, from which it already fills `AnalysisState.settled` (`src/ui/useAnalysis.ts`). `AnalysisState` gains the optional field `settledBoard?: Board`, filled from `completed.board` whenever `settled` is filled, so the held board's `mePlayerId` is the seat the held rows were ranked for.
8. `HighlightMark.kind` (`src/ui/store.ts`) gains `'ring'`. `contestMarks(contested: RowContest['contested'], colorOf: (playerId: string) => string): HighlightMark[]` in `src/ui/analysisMarks.ts` returns one `{ ref, color, kind: 'ring' }` per contested spot, in spot order, in the colour of the first rival listed for that spot. `AnalysisPanel` appends these after `recommendationMarks(...)` through one local helper used by both the hover preview and the selection, so a ring is drawn above the row's other marks. `recommendationMarks`, the resting marks (`src/ui/restMarks.ts`) and the likely-gone marks do not change and carry no ring.
9. `markLayer` (`src/ui/BoardCanvas.tsx`) draws a ring mark as a group with `pointerEvents="none"` and key `hl:ring:${ref}`, holding two circles centred on the corner with `fill="none"`: first class `vertex-ring-edge` with stroke `#30271f` and stroke width 5, then class `vertex-ring` with stroke `mark.color` and stroke width 3. Both circles have `r="16"`, or `r="19"` when the marks being drawn also hold a vertex mark with the same `ref`, so that a gap shows between the ring and a faded second-pick circle on the same corner (radius 12, outer edge 13.2; owner, 2026-10-13). `markLayer` decides this from the list it is drawing and no field is added to `HighlightMark` for it. It has no label. The key differs from a vertex mark's `hl:${ref}` because a row's faded second-pick circle can sit on one of its own spots. A ring is not in `highlightSet`, so a ring by itself hides no legal-corner dot: the dot inside it stays drawn and tappable. A corner that also carries one of the row's vertex marks (the faded second-pick circle) keeps today's rule, and that mark hides the dot. A ring never lands on a building, because a spot has none.
10. Every listed row whose `contested` is not empty shows one `<span className="analysis-contest">` (owner, 2026-10-13: short wording, rivals combined, on a line of its own).
    - Text. The contesting rivals are grouped by `k`, the number of the row's spots that rival contests; groups are ordered by their first rival in roster order, and names inside a group are in roster order. Each group is one clause: `{Names} can reach {k} of {m} spots`, where `m` is the row's number of spots and `Names` is `A`, `A and B`, or `A, B and C` (commas, then ` and ` before the last). When `m` is 1 the clause is `{Names} can reach the only spot`. Clauses are joined by `; ` and nothing follows the last. Examples: `Blue and White can reach 1 of 2 spots`; `Blue can reach 2 of 2 spots; White can reach 1 of 2 spots`; `Red can reach the only spot`. A name is the rival's name on the board the contest was computed from. ASCII only.
    - Position. The span is a child of the row's button (`.analysis-row-select`), after `.analysis-score` and before `.analysis-factors`. It is not inside `.analysis-row-body`, so the first-pick tiles and the `then ... or ...` tiles stay centred on each other exactly as today, and the note sits below both and above the factor breakdown of a selected row. A row with no contest renders no such element.
    - Style. `src/ui/editor.css` gains two rules beside the other `.pick-ledger` row rules: `.analysis-contest { color: var(--muted); font-size: 11px; line-height: 1.35; overflow-wrap: anywhere; }` and `.pick-ledger .analysis-contest { grid-column: 2 / -1; }`, the columns `.pick-ledger .analysis-factors` already spans. `.analysis-contest` joins the selector list of the `.analysis-stale` rule that already paints `.analysis-availability` as a tint block. The note wraps and is never clipped or shortened.
11. Both trees get the note and the rings through the shared `AnalysisPanel` and `BoardCanvas`. Nothing under `src/ui/phone/` changes and no rule is added inside `@media (max-width: 760px)`.

Documentation: in `.claude/specs/desktop/spec.md` the bullets `Selecting a row` and `Ranked rows` of section D4 (Analysis panel), in `.claude/specs/mobile/spec.md` the bullets on ranked recommendations and on tapping a row of section S5 (Analysis block), and the sentence in both that legal-corner dots hide under highlight marks, each say what the ring and the note are and that a ring by itself hides no dot. ## What the label does not show

- A row's expansion spots are the corners one road past the row's road. They are not the two sites the engine's expansion price sums: that price also looks two paid roads out and behind the pick's other two edges. The row's road is the first edge of a cheapest path to the engine's best site, so when that site is two edges from the pick and the path to it is those two edges, it is one of the row's spots.
- A row whose road opens only corners further out has no spot, and so no ring and no note. The engine returns a road only when it can reach a site along it, so every row with a road and no spot is this case: 91 of 11,998 listed rows with a road in the appendix.
- A rival's predicted pick is not a contest until it is on the board (requirement 6).

## Edge cases

- A spot that is itself a rival's road end is contested by that rival.
- A road whose far end carries a building makes no road end there. A corner next to that building is not a spot either, by the building test in requirement 3.
- A connecting edge owned by a third seat stops the rival behind it. The third seat contests the spot, because the spot is then a road end of the third seat. A connecting edge owned by the viewed seat stops every rival behind it.
- Two rivals on one spot: one ring, in the colour of the rival earlier in the roster, and one clause naming both when they contest the same number of spots. One rival on both spots: two rings and the one clause `{Name} can reach 2 of 2 spots`.
- A rival road that ends on `n`, the far end of the row's road, lies on an edge from `n` to one of the two corners past it, so that corner is not a spot. The rival contests the other corner if it is a spot, and the note then reads `{Name} can reach the only spot`.
- One spot: the note reads `{Names} can reach the only spot`. No spot (`firstRoad` null because `expansionWeight` is 0 or the pick is boxed in, both edges past `n` owned by rivals, or both corners barred by buildings): no ring and no note.
- A row whose pick the likely-gone line blocks, a row with no line ahead of it, and a row behind a long line all follow the same rule, because the line is not read.
- A rival road placed by hand counts exactly as one laid by `Place settlement` or `Play these out`. A road in the likely-gone line does not count until it is placed.
- The viewed player differing from the claimed player: the rows, rings and notes are the viewed player's, and the claimed player is named in a note like any other rival. A board with no claimed player shows the claim row and no rows, so no note.
- A setup road never sits on an edge the label needs. A rival road on the row's road would put that rival's settlement on the pick or next to it, which makes the pick illegal. A rival road on an edge from `n` to a corner would put the settlement on `n`, again next to the pick, or on the corner, which is then not a spot. The rival-owned-edge tests in requirement 3 therefore only fire for roads placed by hand or imported, and the appendix found none in 11,998 rows of engine-played drafts.

## Non-goals

- Any change to a score, a ranking or the road a row draws.
- Rivals two roads away, the Rust scorer, the simulator and `engine.wasm`.
- Ringing a spot the engine's price sums through a different first edge than the row's road, or more than one road past it. Such a spot is priced and not ringed.
- Contests in Best moves after the draft.
- Reading or drawing the likely-gone line's pieces in a row's marks.

## Alternatives considered

- The engine returns the sites its price sums (a new `expansionSites` field written in `simulator/crates/engine/src/placement/analysis/rank.rs` beside `first_road`). It makes the ring agree with the price exactly. It needs a reference walk exposed outside tests, an `engine.wasm` rebuild, a regenerated `simulator/fixtures/analysis-parity.json` re-verified by hand, new `analyze` rows in `bench-hashes.json` and cargo tests in both profiles, and it reopens the owner's decision. Not taken unless the owner asks for it.
- Reading the likely-gone line as well as the board. A ring could then point at a rival road the board does not draw, unless each row's marks also drew the line's pieces, which changes what a row shows beyond this spec.
- Filling the contest into each `Recommendation` in `analysis.worker.ts`. Cached rankings would then carry it, but `Recommendation` would stop mirroring the engine's JSON and every test that builds one would change.
- Looking two paid roads out when a road has no spot one road out. It would cover the 91 rows above at the cost of a second rule and a note that no longer means within one road.
