# Placement programme

Class **D**: decisions, their rationale, and what each revision replaced. Not falsifiable by code. See [spec.md](spec.md) for the class rules.

This programme sharpens the **settlement placement scorer**: `valuation.ts` and `weights.ts` in `src/engine/`, and their Rust mirror `app_formula.rs`. It is a second programme, not a revision of [programme.md](programme.md), which ran phases A through J plus knight timing and closed at Phase I adoption. That programme's subject was mostly the opponent model and in-game policy, but not entirely: its H1 block screened the existing placement weights (M-38, M-39, M-40) and its Phase I adoption changed one of them. Those readings are inherited here, not superseded, and the placement-weight prior H1 left open is a decision SP6 made and made against the prior: M-62 re-read `diversityWeight` at 4.8 on the draft-aware field at `+0.302pp` `equivalent` and dropped it, so the axis keeps its shipped 1.6. [programme.md](programme.md)'s H and I entries own both. This one's subject is which two vertices you take.

Phases are numbered **SP0 through SP6**, kept distinct from the old series because its letters and its roman-numeral Phase I mean something else. **Every phase has run and the programme is closed**, so each phase description below is a record of what ran rather than a plan, and the SP6 completion note at the end of this file is the last word. New readings append to [measurements.md](measurements.md) in the existing M numbering. The surfaces this programme closes are filed as gaps: `SIM-GAP-20` and `SIM-GAP-34` through `SIM-GAP-37` in [gaps.md](gaps.md), of which `SIM-GAP-20` closed at SP5 and `SIM-GAP-34` at the SP6 adoption, both deleted, leaving `SIM-GAP-35` through `SIM-GAP-37` open on terms that exist in both scorers and ship at 0. `SIM-GAP-38` through `SIM-GAP-40` were also this programme's, and SP1 closed and deleted them; see the SP1 completion note below. SP4 is the exception and closes no gap of its own: it reweights terms the formula already has.

## Four inherited constraints fix the order

**A field change invalidates every result measured against the old field.** This is the reordering principle [programme.md](programme.md) records at length, and it is why tuning ran last there. It has a consequence that looks strange on a placement programme's face: **the in-game robber and knight work leads, in SP1, before any placement term is touched.** Those changes move the field. Landing them after the placement sweeps would invalidate the sweeps.

The same rule runs backwards, and this programme inherits the consequence rather than escaping it: **SP1 invalidates the Phase-I vector adopted at M-46 as a confirmed result.** Those defaults stay shipped, but the reading that confirmed them describes the pre-SP1 field, and [programme.md](programme.md)'s Phase I entry already records that a field change re-opens them. So SP1 ends with a re-screen on `tuning`, before SP2 opens, and every SP2-through-SP6 reading is measured against the re-screened field. The re-screen covers `handValueWeight` as well as the adopted policy axes: the reading behind it, M-43 in [measurements.md](measurements.md), was taken on a field playing ETW-aware, which is the property SP1 changes. `SIM-GAP-33` was the other pending field change, and it rides SP1: the tightened contest block-bonus predicate lands first among SP1's behaviour changes, so its own A/B reads against the adopted defaults and no later placement result is taken on a field it can still move.

**Measure the composite, not the singles.** Every phase below runs paired increment A/Bs against a fixed full-composite reference, and SP6 is not optional. M-21 and M-44 in [measurements.md](measurements.md) are the readings that forced this rule; read both before designing any arm here.

**Every formula term costs more than one edit.** [contracts.md](contracts.md) owns what a placement-formula change has to touch, under "Acceptance for any engine change" and "Weights files"; read both before costing a term. The decision that belongs here, and all of it: the cost is paid per term rather than per phase, which is why the phase list prefers few terms with swept weights over many terms.

**The shared formula stays deterministic.** The app runs a rollout layer above the formula (draft-order inference, opponent sampling, survival weighting); the simulator's setup path has none, and giving it one is SP5. So a formula term may read only board state, current occupancy, and quantities derived from those. Draft slot qualifies, which is what admits SP4: the draft is a fixed snake, so the pick index follows from the number of settlements already placed, and the picking seat's slot follows from the pick index. Deriving it is not the same as having it, though. Neither scorer takes a slot today (both take a seat only to reach its holdings), so SP4 pays for adding the argument to both signatures and to the parity fixture. Anything probabilistic about what opponents will take stays in the app's rollout layer, above the parity boundary. This is a design decision with teeth: it is what lets SP3 ship an expansion term without first closing `SIM-GAP-20`.

## Phases

Every measured item below preregisters an equivalence threshold and the paired units needed to resolve it, and every item is disposed of by the same rule, using the verdicts [contracts.md](contracts.md) defines. `better` keeps the term at its measured value; `worse` drops it and the phase records why it was expected to help; `equivalent` at declared power drops it and records the null as an answer. `inconclusive` means the arm was under-powered and answers nothing: buy units once, at four times the boards (`--boards`, not `--reps`, per the paired-statistics contract), and if it is still `inconclusive` record the item unresolved and stop. That cap is preregistered so no item can absorb runs indefinitely. **A term survives to SP6 only if it came back `better`.** An unresolved item is not a survivor, and neither is one nobody measured. The rule governs optional terms; it does not govern the gap-closing work in SP1 and SP5, which lands on the gap-fix rule whatever its A/B reads.

- **SP0 — Diagnostics.** Two readings on `tuning` seeds, each of which decides whether a later phase runs at all. Diagnostics come first because this programme's predecessor repeatedly read under-powered singles as null results; knowing an effect's size before designing its A/B is the cheapest fix for that. Both run on `standard4` at four seats, 2000 boards x 2 reps on `tuning`, every seat on the composite policy, and both report per draft slot so a slot effect cannot hide inside an average. Both have the same shape: a quantity read off the board at setup time, joined to how that game ended, a win or the round cap. Each diagnostic says below exactly when its quantity is read; neither reads it at game end.

  **D1, coastal selection.** Over every setup pick the current formula makes, take the chosen vertex and the best-scoring unchosen legal alternative whose pip total is within one of it, and record the hex count of each. Discard the pairs whose hex counts are equal: a tie expresses no preference and only dilutes the share. Output: over the pairs that remain, the share in which the formula took the lower hex count, and the win rate of the games split by that choice. Passing condition for SP2c: that share sits above 50% by more than its interval, clustered by board and taken at the run's declared `alpha` as the paired-statistics contract in [contracts.md](contracts.md) requires, **and** those games win at least 1pp less often. Both, not either; a preference the win rate does not punish is not a bias worth a term. Where each figure comes from, since a gate that decides whether a term gets built should not turn on a number someone picked: 50% is the null the construction forces, a formula blind to hex count orders a pip-matched set independently of hex count, which leaves the chosen vertex and the runner-up exchangeable in it, so a real preference is any reliable departure from even odds and the interval is what makes "reliable" decidable. The 1pp is a registered threshold, which is a decision this file is entitled to make and does not have to derive; what it must not be is discovered after the run. This sizes `SIM-GAP-35`.

  **D2, expansion and blockability.** Two readings from one run, because the phases they gate need different quantities. Both quantities are measured on the pair the moment it is completed, which is the setup-time surface `SIM-GAP-34` and the dropped robber-attraction term are both about; measuring either at game end would price sixty turns of opponent play instead. Both are then joined to the game's outcome and reported the same way, as the win rate of the top quartile minus the win rate of the bottom quartile of that quantity, so the two magnitudes are constructed alike and the branch below compares like with like. *Boxing:* the number of legal expansion sites the completed pair can still reach within two road builds. This sizes `SIM-GAP-34` and fixes the units SP3's A/B needs to resolve `equivalent` rather than `inconclusive` at the effect size it finds. Report the incidence of zero reachable sites alongside it, since that is the case a threshold-shaped response would live in. *Blockability:* the share of the pair's total pips sitting on its single highest-pip hex. The robber-attraction term dropped below is revisited only if the blockability quartile gap is the larger of the two in magnitude; without this second reading that condition cannot be evaluated at all, which is why it is specified here rather than left to the phase that reads it. A quantity whose quarter cuts coincide counts as a magnitude of zero on either side of that comparison: its arms overlap, so its gap contrasts a set with a superset of itself and is an artifact rather than a separation. Neither quantity was degenerate on M-48, so this carve-out does not touch what that run read.
- **SP1 — Robber and knight (the field change).** Five items in dependency order. **SP1a:** put `threat.rs::own_need_hit` inside the quantity `threat.rs::robber_choice` maximizes, closing the wiring half of `SIM-GAP-38`. That is the whole behaviour change, and the phrasing matters because the restructuring it is usually described as is a no-op: the hex loop already maximizes a term carrying the best available victim's rank, and the victim loop then picks that same victim, so rewriting the two-stage search as a joint argmax over (hex, victim) pairs returns the identical choice on today's terms. Do the rewrite anyway, for the shape SP1b needs and because a joint maximum subsumes both orderings, so "sometimes choose the victim first, then block as much as they hold" needs no mode switch and no second code path. Do not measure it on its own: an A/B of the restructuring alone is guaranteed to read `equivalent` and would be a null about nothing. **SP1b:** price denial of the victim's own need in `threat.rs::victim_rank`, closing the rest of `SIM-GAP-38`, through the one opponent-threat function rather than a parallel model. **SP1c:** a completion step in the need model, closing `SIM-GAP-40`. **SP1d:** promote the knight's own-tile relief constant to a declared, bounds-checked parameter, closing `SIM-GAP-39`. **SP1e:** sweep `knightStealWeight`, `knightPlacementWeight` and SP1d's new axis. SP1e runs last within the phase for a reason worth stating: sweeping the weight on a signal that cannot reach the decision it scales measures nothing, which is the state SP1a found.
- **SP2 — Cheap formula terms.** **SP2a:** a dev-card recipe bonus over ore, wheat and sheep, closing `SIM-GAP-36`. It mirrors the three existing recipe terms exactly, so it is the lowest-cost term in the programme, and it is the only one that raises sheep's standing on its own. **SP2b:** condition port value on coverage deficit, closing `SIM-GAP-37`, so a port pays most where it converts a narrow spread and least where it duplicates production the pair already has. **SP2c:** a tempo term sensitive to hex count independent of pips, closing `SIM-GAP-35`. SP2c runs only if SP0-D1 meets its stated passing condition.
- **SP3 — Expansion.** One term, closing `SIM-GAP-34`, not two. Being boxed in is expansion potential equal to zero, so a separate boxing penalty would double-count on the same vertices and neither weight would be attributable. The merge assumes the response is a gradient; if SP0-D2 finds boxing is a threshold effect that only bites at zero sites, one linear term cannot carry it and the penalty comes back as its own item. Quality-weighted rather than counted: the value of the best sites a settlement opens within two road builds, discounted per build, using the existing vertex base score. `policy::frontier::opened` is the in-game measure of the same quantity and is the reference implementation for what "opened" means, but it counts vertices without regard to their worth. M-36 is the reading that will be cited against this phase; read the entry itself before citing it, and see the design note below for why it does not settle a quality-weighted setup term. Ships with the road-direction recommendation in the app, which falls out of the same walk at no extra modelling cost and is what makes the term actionable to a user.
- **SP4 — Draft-slot conditioning.** The first and last seats face different problems: the last picks a planned pair back to back, the first takes the best single vertex and then takes what survives `2 * (seats - 1)` intervening picks, six at four seats. So the weight set is keyed on the slot and the seat count together, not on the slot alone. Slot is derivable on both sides of the parity boundary but is plumbed to neither scorer today, which is the phase's real cost; see the determinism constraint above. Kept as a small keyed weight set, deliberately not a general conditional-weight framework: the framework is only worth building once a second conditioning variable has earned its place.
- **SP5 — Setup draft awareness, then denial.** Closes `SIM-GAP-20` by giving the Rust setup path an opponent model, then adds a denial term. Denial splits into two cases that this programme deliberately treats differently. *Incidental* denial is partly reached today, and by a different mechanism than it looks: the app's rollout layer weights a candidate by the chance it survives to your pick, which prices your risk of losing a spot, not the value a rival loses when you take it. Nothing in either scorer credits the second thing, so a denial term is a real addition rather than a duplicate. *Sacrificial* denial, taking a worse spot to hurt a specific rival, pays only through win probability, and at setup there is no leader to aim at, while in a four-player game the damage is shared with two seats you are not targeting. **Preregister the expectation that its weight is at or near zero.** A preregistered null is only worth anything if it can fail, so the preregistration must name the equivalence threshold and the paired units needed to resolve `equivalent` at that threshold. `equivalent` at declared power confirms the expectation; `inconclusive` confirms nothing and means the arm was under-powered. Largest lift and lowest expected payoff in the programme, so it goes last. The denial term may be cut, but only on a reading that measures denial: SP0 measures none, so nothing in SP0 licenses cutting it, and the opponent-model half is never cuttable because closing `SIM-GAP-20` is the phase's point.
- **SP6 — Combine and confirm.** A combined arm over every surviving term, then a coordinate pass that attempts to remove each one at the combined point. H4 is the precedent and the warning: read M-44 for what a coordinate pass did to a list of screened winners. Confirmation runs on a clean domain; see the open decision below. As run, the re-screen against the new field left one survivor, so there was no combined arm and no coordinate pass to make: the confirmation took `eval2` and the adoption gate `gate2`, the clean domains that decision was resolved into.

## Interview verdicts on the proposed changes

Recorded because the reasoning outlives the phase list, and two of the five proposals changed shape on contact with the code.

**Boxing in and expansion potential are one item.** Merged into SP3 for the double-counting and attribution reasons given there.

**Denial is real but structurally last.** SP5 records the split and the preregistered near-zero expectation. The specific proposal of taking a port to deny a rival's strategy is the weakest instance: a port that does not match your own production costs you a hex to deny a plan you inferred.

**The knight proposal was already built, and mismeasured rather than missing.** `threat.rs::robber_choice` and `heuristic_v1.rs::knight_action_score` already price the victim through belief and the shared danger model, and M-31 measured the result. What was actually open is `SIM-GAP-38` through `SIM-GAP-40`, which is why SP1 is a wiring and parameter phase rather than a feature phase. The user's ruling stands that this is gameplay rather than settlement choice; it leads this programme only because of the field-before-formula rule above.

**`handValue` is not the lever, and re-litigating M-43 is not this programme's job.** M-43 in [measurements.md](measurements.md) answered that axis and Phase I in [programme.md](programme.md) disposed of it; nothing here reopens either. This previously said the term was settled and gave domain cost as the reason: the cost claim was wrong, and "settled" is now qualified by the SP1 re-screen above, which reprices the axis against the moved field. The mechanism behind the concern is nonetheless real and now has its own id: the setup grant is one card per adjacent producing hex regardless of token, so the dropped term was the only place hex count entered the formula independently of pip total (`SIM-GAP-35`). SP2c is therefore a differently specified term measured on fresh evidence, not a re-litigation of M-43, and SP0-D1 gates it.

**Four additions were adopted into the phase list** beyond the five proposals: the dev-card recipe (SP2a), coverage-conditioned ports (SP2b), draft-slot conditioning (SP4), and the road-direction output (SP3). One was considered and dropped: a robber-attraction term pricing a pair whose economy sits on one blockable high-pip hex. `duplicateNumberPenalty` was the first reason given and is the wrong one: it scores the overlap between token numbers the pair already holds and token numbers the candidate adds, so two separate hexes carrying the same number are penalised exactly like one shared hex, though only the shared hex falls to a single robber, and production piled on one hex with no repeated number is not penalised at all. The real reason is that the effect is unmeasured and the term is speculative next to five that are not. SP0-D2's blockability reading is what would change that, under the branch stated there.

## Seed domains: open decision

[programme.md](programme.md)'s seed-domain section records what each existing domain has been spent on and the rule for when a spent domain stays usable; the repo `CLAUDE.md` forbids `eval` and `gate` commands outright. Read that rule and it might admit the existing domains for the terms here. This programme does not lean on it.

**Recommendation: mint new domain constants for this programme**, extending the existing disjointness assertion. The claim is then unambiguous and no future reader has to reconstruct which parameters a domain has seen. **This decision is not blocking.** SP0 through SP5 run entirely on `tuning`; only SP6's confirmation and any adoption gate need a clean domain. Resolve it before SP6, not before SP0.

## Design notes that outlive their phase

**One term per concept.** Applied twice in this file: expansion and boxing merged, and denial folded into one victim model rather than a second. It was almost applied a third time, to the robber-attraction term, and that reading was wrong: two terms that sound alike are not one quantity, and the verdict above records how to tell. Two terms describing one quantity make both weights unattributable, which is the same failure the predecessor programme recorded for lexicographic tie-break ladders.

**A count is not a quality measure, and a result about one is not a result about the other.** M-36 is the reading at issue; [measurements.md](measurements.md) owns what it found, and two things about its shape limit how far it reaches. It intervened on a count, mid-game. SP3 is a quality measure, at setup, on an empty board where the fan of reachable sites is at its widest. Neither difference is a reason to expect the same answer, so read the entry before citing it and do not cite it against SP3.

**The parity boundary decides where a signal can live.** Deterministic and board-derived, it can be a formula term in both scorers. Probabilistic about opponents, it belongs in the app's rollout layer until SP5 gives the Rust side an equivalent. A term that needs opponent sampling to be computed at all is an SP5 item by construction, whatever phase it is proposed in.

## SP0 decisions, 2026-09-01

Recorded after the SP0 diagnostic ran. Both readings come from one `diagnose` invocation on `tuning`, `standard4`, four seats, 2000 boards x 2 reps, every seat on the composite policy with player trading and scoring placements through the app formula at `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`; the numbers, their provenance and their limits live in M-47 and M-48 in [measurements.md](measurements.md), and the two preregistrations that fixed the conditions before the run are `docs/plans/preregs/2026-09-01-m47-sp0-d1-coastal-selection.md` and `docs/plans/preregs/2026-09-01-m48-sp0-d2-expansion-blockability.md`. These are decisions the run made; they supersede the conditionals above rather than correcting them, and each says which one it resolves.

**SP2c does not run, and `SIM-GAP-35` closes as not indicated rather than unresolved.** This resolves the SP2 bullet's "SP2c runs only if SP0-D1 meets its stated passing condition". The gate failed its first condition and failed it in the informative direction: among pip-matched candidate pairs the shipped formula takes the *higher* hex count 54.8% of the time, interval `[43.57%, 46.79%]` on the lower-hex share, entirely below the 50% the construction makes the null. The second condition passed, at +5.01pp rather than the 1pp registered, and it passes the same way: taking the lower hex count is what associates with losing. So the formula's residual preference and the outcome contrast point the same way, and there is no concentration bias for a hex-count tempo term to correct. The mechanism `SIM-GAP-35` names is still real, the setup grant pays per producing hex regardless of token and `handValueWeight` is 0, but the diversity, coverage, duplicate-number and port terms already lean the formula toward hex count rather than against it. What the reading does not license, and what the gap entry must not be read as saying: that a hex-count term would be worthless. It says nothing about a term's value, only that the bias it was proposed to correct points the other way, so the phase that would build it has no evidence behind it. Anyone reopening this needs a differently specified term and a fresh reading, not a re-reading of M-47.

**SP3 stays one gradient term. No separate boxing penalty.** This resolves the SP3 bullet's hedge, "if SP0-D2 finds boxing is a threshold effect that only bites at zero sites, one linear term cannot carry it and the penalty comes back as its own item", and the matching hedge in the "Boxing in and expansion potential are one item" verdict. Zero of 16,000 completed pairs had no reachable expansion site, so at four seats on `standard4` the threshold case does not occur at pair completion at all and there is no zero-site population for a separate penalty to price. The response over the observed range of 1 to 20 sites is a gradient worth +6.46pp between the outer quartiles pooled, +5.54pp to +13.95pp by slot. The scope of that answer is exactly the run: it does not extend to `extension6` or to other seat counts, and SP3 should power its arm off a per-slot magnitude, because the quantity's scale moves with board fill and the pooled row mixes four fill levels.

**The robber-attraction term is revisited, as a separate item inside SP3's scope, and is not built yet.** This supersedes the "one was considered and dropped" verdict above, on the branch that verdict itself named: the blockability quartile gap is the larger of the two in magnitude, `-11.37pp` against boxing's `+6.46pp`, negative at every slot. The term was dropped for being speculative next to five that are not, and it is no longer speculative. It is also not yet specifiable, for a reason M-48 records and this note repeats because it is the first thing SP3 has to deal with: blockability and hex count are mechanically entangled, since the share on a pair's top hex has a floor near one over the number of hexes it touches, so the concentrated quartile is enriched in low-hex-count pairs, which M-47 found win about 5pp less often. Nothing in SP0 separates "one robber takes this economy away" from "this pair touches few hexes". A term specified before that separation would price hex count under another name, on the same surface SP2c was just closed over, and would land two terms on one quantity, which is the failure the "one term per concept" note above exists to prevent. So the item is open with its first task named, not scheduled.

**What SP0 did not measure, restated so nothing borrows its authority.** Both readings are observational: the quantity is the formula's own choice or the pair's own shape, never an intervention, so every gap here sizes an association and none is a causal effect of moving a weight. The D2 gaps carry no interval and no significance claim by preregistration. Nothing in SP0 measures denial, so nothing here touches SP5, which the SP5 bullet already states.

## SP1 completion, 2026-09-01

**SP1 is complete and the field has moved.** Five behaviour changes landed: the robber picks hex and victim by one joint argmax (SP1a), the victim's own need is priced through `victimNeedWeight` (SP1b), the need model has a completion step through `needCompletionWeight` (SP1c), the knight's own-tile relief constant is the declared parameter `knightReliefWeight` at its former literal value (SP1d), and the `SIM-GAP-33` contest block-bonus predicate now requires that the rival actually needs a new road. `SIM-GAP-33`, `SIM-GAP-38`, `SIM-GAP-39` and `SIM-GAP-40` are deleted from [gaps.md](gaps.md). M-49 measured the predicate correction as costless and M-50 swept the five threat axes to a clean sheet of nulls, so no default moved out of SP1 beyond SP1d's behaviour-neutral promotion.

**SP1a shipped narrower than the SP1 bullet above specified, and the residue is re-filed as `SIM-GAP-41`.** That bullet named SP1a as putting `threat.rs::own_need_hit` inside the quantity `threat.rs::robber_choice` maximizes. What landed is the joint argmax over `(hex, victim)` pairs and nothing else: `own_need_hit` still reaches only the winning pair, through `RobberChoice::steal_value`. The substitution was deliberate, and its reason is the corpus discipline this programme runs on. Moving that term into the maximized quantity moves hex and victim choice, so SP1a would stop being the behaviour-neutral restructure the bullet allows it to land as without a measurement of its own, and it would move the field under M-49, which had to read the `SIM-GAP-33` predicate against the pre-SP1b defaults. What SP1a and SP1b together did close of `SIM-GAP-38` is its victim half: the victim is chosen jointly with the hex rather than taken from an already-chosen one, and `victimNeedWeight` prices the victim's own need inside `victim_rank`, which the maximized quantity carries. The observer half stayed open, so deleting `SIM-GAP-38` whole overstated what SP1 closed; [gaps.md](gaps.md) now carries the remainder as `SIM-GAP-41`. Landing it is a field change and needs a preregistered A/B of its own, so it is open and unscheduled rather than pending inside SP1, and every reading from M-49 onwards is taken on the field without it.

**The field's provenance is on the record before SP2 opens.** SP1 invalidated the M-46 vector as a confirmed result, because a measurement is a statement about the field it was taken on. M-51 re-screened all five disposals against the post-SP1 defaults at confirmation power and every one still reads the way M-43 through M-46 recorded it: `trading.etwWeight`, `trading.dangerFloor` and `trading.dangerWeight` each cost about 3pp to revert (`worse`), `devBuyScale` costs `-1.10pp` with its interval straddling the practical threshold (direction confirmed, magnitude recorded unresolved with no retry, by preregistration), and restoring `handValueWeight` reads `+0.017pp` (`equivalent`, so the drop holds). No arm read `better`, so nothing is flagged for the user and no default moved. The two axes whose support strengthened are `trading.dangerFloor` and `trading.dangerWeight`, which M-44 had kept on the parsimony rule against slightly favourable removal estimates and which now earn their values directly.

**Every SP2 reading below is measured against this re-screened field.** The post-SP1 composite defaults, `simulator/placement/policy-default-params.json` as of this note, are the reference for M-52 onwards, and M-51 is the entry that establishes what they are worth. Two limits transfer with them: M-51's `handValueWeight` arm was taken on an app-formula field where M-43's was taken on `pip_diversity`, so the two readings differ in field policy and field placement at once and no difference between them may be attributed to SP1 alone; and each M-51 contrast puts one reverted value on the hero seat against three seats at defaults, which is not a measurement of a field where every seat reverts. M-46's package estimate stands unchanged as the last word on the Phase-I vector as a package, and all three seed domains stay spent for it.

## SP2 terms as built, 2026-09-01

## SP2a verdict, 2026-09-01
