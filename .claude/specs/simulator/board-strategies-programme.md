# Board-specific strategies programme

Vet: 6/10 staged-build 2026-10-13 pair: .claude/pairs/board-strategies-programme sha: 85e18839

## Summary

The cut-off programme ([`cut-off-programme.md`](cut-off-programme.md)) measured two placement weights as blanket settings and adopted nothing. `contestWeight` discounts an expansion spot a rival can reach, and is off at 0. `repriceFirstExpansion` is a switch that re-prices a first pick after later rivals have placed. The best arm gained about +0.6pp of win rate at four and six seats against a +0.5pp bar, and the runs were too small to tell the two apart. The owner's decisions (2026-10-13) are to keep both weights, to apply a strategy only in the situations where it helps, to treat this as the first of several board-specific strategies, and to size every run in this programme generously. The work is staged so each stage is cheap and decides whether the next is worth its price:

1. Settle the known gain: one large held-out run of the always-on setting at four and six seats, after one evaluator change that lets a run of that size fit in memory.
2. Build a per-game record and a rule search, and look for the situations where a setting helps.
3. Build a race-aware form of the discount, which concedes a spot only to a rival who would get there first, when the record shows road readiness matters. Dropped by the owner on 2026-10-14 after the diagnostic (Decisions).

A situation is what is known before a pick is chosen: the seat count, the picking seat's draft position, and features of the board's terrain. A rule is a condition on a situation plus the weight leaves to apply when it holds. A strategy is one arm and one rule.

The owner's race requirement (2026-10-13): a spot is conceded only to a rival who would get there first. A rival whose second settlement pays brick and wood, or who can trade for the missing one, builds a road on its first turn and cuts the spot off, so the spot is worth less. A seat that moves earlier and holds the road itself gets there first, so the spot keeps its value. The flat discount cannot tell these cases apart, because the scorer merges every rival into one set of masks.

## Requirements

### Terms

- **Unit:** one board, one hero seat, one rep, as `evaluation_schedule` (`simulator/crates/cli/src/evaluate.rs` 328) lists them: boards ascending, then reps, then hero seats. `evaluation_unit_config` (602) derives a unit's game seed from the domain, board, rep and hero seat and not from the arm or from the unit's place in the run, so two arms whose hero drafts the same pieces play the same game, and a unit plays the same game in a run that plays every unit as in a run that plays some.
- **Draft position (`slot`):** the hero seat's index, 0 for the first picker. `setup_order` (`simulator/crates/engine/src/game.rs` 1547) gives seat `i` pick `i` of the first round and pick `2n - 1 - i` overall for its second, and the turn loop (`game.rs` 599) runs seats from 0.
- **Shipped vector:** `placement/seat-weights/seats-<n>.json` as it stands when a run is preregistered. Every run's field and `base` are the shipped vector, so a stage 1 adoption changes what later runs at that seat count compare against.
- **Arms:** each arm is a weights file `placement/arms/co_<label>_s<n>.json`, which is seat count `n`'s anchor vector plus that arm's leaves. The anchor vector is `seats-<n>.json` as it stood before the cut-off programme, frozen in `CO_ANCHORS` (`simulator/crates/engine/src/policy/params_file.rs` 1777). `flat_75` sets `contestWeight` 0.75. `flat_reprice` sets `repriceFirstExpansion` 1 with `contestWeight` 0.75 at three, four and six seats and 0.5 at five. `off` sets nothing. `race_reprice` and `race_75` were item 6's (requirement 21) and were never built; the `slice` test that names them uses them only as column labels.
- **Held-out domain:** `gate5`, `eval5` and `gate6`. `tuning5` and `tuning6` are screening domains. All five are spend-once.
- **Fired unit:** a unit whose situation satisfies a rule. On every other unit a strategy's hero uses the shipped vector, so the unit is an exact tie with `base`. A situation is the same at both of a hero's picks, so a rule fires for both or for neither.
- **Fired board:** a board with at least one fired unit. Every rule in this spec is a condition on `slot` (the same seats on every board) joined with a condition on terrain (the whole board or none of it), so every fired board holds the same number of fired units.
- **Road readiness:** how soon a seat can build its first road, read from the cards its second settlement is paid: one card for each hex the settlement touches that produces a resource (`grant_setup_resources`, `game.rs` 554). The bank never limits this grant: one seat takes at most three cards of a resource, so six seats take at most 18, against a bank of 19 on `standard4` and 24 on `extension6` (`rules.rs` 234). Level 2: the cards include at least one brick and at least one wood. Level 1: the cards include brick or wood but not both, and number two or more, so one is spare to trade for the missing one. Level 0: otherwise.
- **Fired gain:** the paired win-rate difference over fired units only, `(b - c) / fired units`, where `b` counts fired units the arm won and `base` lost and `c` the reverse. **Total gain:** the fired gain times the share of units that fire, which is `(b - c) / all units`. Two rows of one units file are therefore ordered by total gain exactly as they are ordered by the integer `b - c`.
- **The bar** (owner, 2026-10-13): a strategy passes a run when its fired-gain interval lies wholly above +0.5pp, which is `pair_verdict` (`evaluate.rs` 707) reading `better` at `--threshold 0.005`. For the always-on rule the fired gain is the gain over all units, which is the cut-off programme's bar unchanged. **The floor:** a rule must fire on at least 20% of units, tested in integers as `5 * fired units >= all units`. `slice` applies it to every rule on the units file it reads, and the confirm and the gate each test it again on their own run (requirements 25 and 26).

### Chunked evaluator (item 2)

6. `evaluate` plays a run in chunks of boards, because today's `evaluate` cannot hold a run of stage 1's size.
   - Why. `prepare_evaluation` (`evaluate.rs` 344) generates every board before the first game and keeps them all, and `prepare_app_formula_boards` (`placement/mod.rs` 122) builds two `AppFormulaScorer`s on every board for the field and for each arm, with no sharing between a field and an arm on the same file (`heuristics.rs` 21). A scorer holds 336 bytes a vertex (`VertexStats`, `app_formula.rs` 414), about 21 KB on `standard4` and 30 KB on `extension6`, and about 28 KB and 41 KB once its bound table is built. A two-arm run therefore holds about 126 KB a board at four seats and 180 KB at six before any game: about 126 GB for 1,000,000 boards and 90 GB for 500,000, on a machine with 24 GB. The saved runs fitted because 22,000 boards is about 3 GB.
   - The change, in `simulator/crates/cli/src/evaluate.rs` alone. `evaluate` calls a new `pub fn evaluate_in_chunks(request, chunk_boards)` with `EVALUATION_CHUNK_BOARDS`, a constant of 1,000.
     - Built once for the whole run, as today: the topology, the rules, the pool, the domain seed, and the full `schedule` from `evaluation_schedule(boards, reps, seats)`, with today's length check.
     - For each chunk of `chunk_boards` consecutive boards in ascending order (the last chunk holds what is left): generate those boards from `mix64(domain_seed ^ board_index)` with the board's index in the whole run, call `prepare_app_formula_boards` on them, build the jobs for every arm and every unit of those boards, play them in the pool, run today's played-exactly-once checks on that chunk's jobs, push each game's win flag and draw flag onto that arm's vectors, add its illegal actions to the total, and drop the boards.
     - A job's `unit_index` stays the unit's index in the full schedule, and a chunk's jobs are ordered by arm and then by unit, so after the last chunk each arm's vectors hold one flag for each unit in schedule order, exactly as today.

Sizing, worked by hand in the confirm's preregistration from the exploration's `slices.json` and one `perf` reading. `h` is half the width of the candidate row's `interval` in `slices.json`, `E` the exploration's board count, and `H0 = 10,000 * ceil(E * (h / 0.0003)^2 / 10,000)`, the smallest multiple of 10,000 boards at which the predicted fired-gain half-width `h * sqrt(E / H0)` is at most 0.03pp.

    - The wall time is read, not scaled from the exploration, whose time holds a different number of arms and no rule. Before the confirm's preregistration is committed, the main session runs the confirm's own command (requirement 25: the two arms, and the rule when it has one) with `--domain perf` at 20,000 boards, and `P` is that run's `elapsedSeconds` in `meta.json`. The predicted wall time of `B` boards is `T(B) = P * B / 20,000`. If the rule fires on no `perf` board the command errors; `P` is then read from the same command without `--where`, which plays more games and so takes longer.
    - `H` is `H1` when `T(H1)` is at most 10,800 seconds, and otherwise `10,000 * floor(216,000,000 / P / 10,000)`, the largest multiple of 10,000 with `T(H)` at most 10,800 seconds. This step never raises `H`. Worked example: `P` of 96 seconds gives 2,250,000, above every `H1`, so `H` is `H1`; `P` of 150 seconds gives 1,440,000, so an `H1` of 2,000,000 becomes 1,440,000 and `T(H)` is 10,800 seconds.
    - `H` is fixed here for the confirm and the gate, and the preregistration states `h * sqrt(E / H)`, the half-width it predicts.
25. Confirm: `base` and the candidate's arm on `eval5` at `H` boards with `--where` set to the candidate's rule text from `slices.json` (no flag for `always`), one invocation. The run passes when its pair's `verdict` is `better` and `config.floorMet` is `true`; a run with no `--where` has no `floorMet` key and meets the floor by definition, since every unit fires. A pass proceeds; anything else stops the seat count on the shipped vector with `gate6` unspent there. A run that ends with requirement 13's no-fired-unit error is a fail of the floor, final by requirement 4, and leaves `eval5` unspent there too. The floor is tested here because the owner's bar names it as part of passing, and a terrain rule's share differs a little from one set of boards to the next.
26. Gate: the same command on `gate6`, one invocation. A pass by requirement 25's test adopts; anything else leaves the shipped vector. Neither held-out domain has a retry, and nothing read on one changes a command run on it. A candidate whose rule is `always` adopts with no amendment: `seats-<n>.json` takes the bytes of the candidate's arm file, its `SEAT_ADOPTIONS` row is set to that arm's leaves over the defaults, and the fixture, wasm, test and timing steps of requirement 7 follow. Any other candidate adopts through requirement 28.

## Design

- Stage 1 comes first because the saved runs say it is the largest gain on offer: conditioning on draft position adds nothing to the total at four and six seats. The chunk change ahead of it is needed by every later run too.
- No rule needs its own game code to be measured. A strategy's hero plays the shipped vector off its rule, so those units are ties by construction, and its result is the arm's result restricted to fired units. The diagnostic run and the exploration therefore play each arm once on every unit and `slice` scores every rule from the one file, and the confirm and the gate play fired units only.
- One estimator serves every reading. `try_paired_stats` needs equal clusters, and every rule here gives them once boards with no fired unit are left out and the rest are renumbered, so `evaluate --where` and `slice` call it with the same inputs and return the same bits. `stats::clustered_mean` handles unequal clusters, but it reaches the same quantity through a different order of floating-point operations, so it is not held to the same bits and would weaken the test that holds the two commands together.
- A rule applies leaves relative to the shipped vector. Where stage 1 adopted, the search runs the other way: the arm is `off`, and a rule names the situations where conceding is switched off.
- Selection is by total gain because that is what a rule is worth to a player; the bar on fired gain is the owner's test of whether a rule is worth applying where it applies. A consequence the owner has seen: a narrow rule passes while adding less overall than a broad one that just misses, which the large run sizes make rare.
- The family is small on purpose: with 85 to 187 rules and up to four arms besides `base` per seat count, the best row is partly luck, and the confirm and the gate are what remove it. A rule whose true fired gain is at or under the bar reads `better` on one held-out run at most 2.5% of the time and on both at most 0.0625%, however it was selected.
- The race readings come from the reference arm's own games, so they cost no extra draft. They are a property of the board and the field: the reference arm's draft does not change with the arm being compared, so slicing an arm's gain by them compares like with like.
- Files: `simulator/crates/cli/src/evaluate.rs` (`EvaluationDomain`, `evaluation_schedule`, `evaluation_unit_config`, `evaluate`, `try_paired_stats`, `pair_verdict`), `simulator/crates/cli/src/main.rs` (flags, the `slice` subcommand), `simulator/crates/cli/src/lib.rs`, new `simulator/crates/cli/src/features.rs` and `slice.rs`, `simulator/crates/engine/src/policy/params_file.rs` (`CO_ARMS`, `SEAT_ADOPTIONS`); for item 6, `simulator/crates/engine/src/placement/expansion.rs` and `app_formula.rs`.

## Edge cases

- A terrain feature whose two cut points are equal yields duplicate predicates; the duplicate-set rule drops them.
- A terrain feature whose smallest value is `c1` makes `f<c1` fire on nothing, which the floor drops, and `f>=c1` fire on everything, which is dropped as a duplicate of `always`.
- A predicate may fire on more than 80% of units. Nothing drops it: the floor is the only limit on share.
- A rule on `slot` alone fires on some seats of every board, so every board is a cluster of `reps` times those seats. A rule on terrain alone fires on every unit of some boards, so those boards are the clusters, each of `reps * seats` units. A joined rule gives the fired boards, each holding `reps` times the fired seats. All three pass `try_paired_stats`' equal-cluster check after renumbering.
- An arm whose column equals the reference's (an arm on the shipped vector) has `b` and `c` of 0 on every rule, an estimate of 0 and an interval of 0 to 0, and never reads `better`.
- Where stage 1 adopted, through items 5 to 9: `base` and the field are the adopted vector; the arms are `base` and `off`; a rule's leaves are `off`'s, the pre-adoption vector; `flat_reprice` equals the shipped vector, so it is not an arm there; `gate5` is spent there and the four new domains are spent as their runs execute.
- A candidate whose rule is `always` runs the held-out commands with no `--where`, and the corner check applies to it.
- A `--where` run whose first chunks hold no fired unit plays nothing in them and continues; only a run with no fired unit at all is an error.
- A held-out run whose fired share falls under 20% fails the floor and stops the seat count, even when its verdict is `better`. At three seats, `slot>=1` joined with a terrain predicate that holds on 30.1% of the exploration's boards fires on 20.07% of units; if it holds on 29.9% of the confirm's boards it fires on 19.93% and the confirm fails.
- A run that leaves no `evaluation.json` spends nothing, and requirement 4's table says what follows each ending: a rule that fired on no unit is final, a wrong preregistration is corrected once, a failed write or a killed process gets one more launch of the same bytes, and a second failure goes to the owner. `slice` refuses a directory with no `evaluation.json`, and refuses a `units.csv` whose row count is not `config.units`.
- A stage 1 run that stops (an illegal action, or `base` off the corner) closes its seat count: no diagnostic, exploration, confirm or gate runs there, so no further domain is spent on an evaluator known to be at fault.
- The same board appears in every seat's units, so a terrain predicate fires for all of a board's units or none.
- Nested rules: when a broad rule has the larger total gain and misses the bar, and a rule inside it passes, the candidate is the narrower rule. The measurement entry's ten rows by total gain show what the broad one would have added.
- A seat whose second settlement touches the desert or fewer than three hexes is paid fewer cards; its readiness is read from the cards it is paid. One card is level 0 whatever it is.
- A second settlement that touches two brick hexes and no wood is level 1: it holds brick and a spare card. One brick hex and one ore hex is level 1. One brick hex alone is level 0.
- `spotFalloff` is a ratio of two integers and is written with Rust's shortest round-trip float text; its cut points are compared as `f64`, and a cut point read back from a rule's text is the same `f64`.
- Measurement numbers: requirement 5's reserved block keeps another session's entries on `main` from taking a number this programme has preregistered.

## Non-goals

- Two seats: no arm read above zero at either position there (M-101, four arms), so nothing is explored.
- Five seats in stage 1, and any always-on run beyond requirement 7.
- More than one rule per seat count, different leaves per rule, rules three predicates deep, a rule family with two predicates on `slot` or two on terrain, and rule conditions on pieces already placed. Each opens as a follow-up only after a rule from this family passes a gate.
- New weight values, any new discount shape (the race rule of requirements 18 to 22 included, since 2026-10-14), a bonus for cutting a rival off, dice production in the first two turns, port and bank trades in road readiness, and the in-game turn policy.
- Re-reading or replaying any spent domain. The two tables in the Summary read saved output files only.
- Slicing arms from earlier programmes. `--units` and `slice` work for any arm, and that is the reuse the owner's direction names; no such run is part of this spec.
- A measurement of the field an adoption creates, with every seat on the adopted strategy.
- Resuming a run that died part way, a marker file for a run in progress, and a check inside `evaluate` for an output directory that exists: requirement 2's guard and requirement 4's rule cover them.
- Sharing scorers between a field and an arm on the same weights file, generating or preparing boards in parallel, and chunking `--picks-only`. Requirement 6 says why the first is left out; chunks alone make every run here fit.
- A library for the rule search. None absorbs the enumeration, and the nearest replaces about 30 lines at the price of a Python toolchain (see Vet).
