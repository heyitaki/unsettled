# Desktop workspace: specification

What the desktop (and, through the shared `Workspace` tree, landscape phone, which is wider than the 760px phone arm) UI must become so it carries the design decisions the portrait phone settled in [`../mobile/spec.md`](../mobile/spec.md). Read that file first: its Surfaces, Behaviours and Decisions are the design; this file says what of it reaches the desktop, what the desktop keeps that the phone dropped, and how the two trees share code afterwards. Where this file is silent, the phone's rendering is the reference: `e2e/phoneShell.spec.ts` produces screenshots of every phone surface under `test-results/phone/`.

Scope is `src/ui/` outside `src/ui/phone/`, the base rules of `src/ui/editor.css`, and the phone files only where a surface is promoted out of them. **The portrait shell must not regress**: every phone e2e test stays green and phone screenshots preserve the layout except for the shared Best picks controls in D4 and the Board tools rows in D3.

## Why

The phone shell was built to a design that fixed decisions the desktop still contradicts: factor text in `{label} {value}` form and autosave already reached both, but the board colour, the claim-on-row roster, the rename-on-name lists, import as the first action, the empty-board state, menus with a check on the current option, and the flat marks in the claimed colour all stopped at the portrait arm. The desktop also keeps two things the phone showed to be wrong: a tab strip as the document switcher (the phone folded open boards into the Maps screen, and landscape phones have had no document switcher at all since the strip was hidden there) and a separate import panel two panels away from the library that receives the import. Desktop and phone share one flat Sand page (`#efe8d9`) and off-red accent (`#cd5037`). Panels have an opaque surface (`#fbf8f1`), a hairline border (`#ddd3bf`), a 6px radius and no shadow. The neutral tint is `#f3eee2`, ink is `#2d2924`, muted text is `#776f64`, grey icons are `#8e867a` (a filled shape reads heavier than lettering, so icons sit a step lighter than muted text; a chevron or sort mark inside a text label takes the label's colour), disabled icons, zero counts and not-yet-playable chips are `#c5bfb5`, the dark accent is `#a33d29`, and danger is `#a33428`. Controls, rows, cards, panels, dialogs and sheets all use one 6px radius (`--radius`); circles and pills stay round, and the board canvas keeps a 14px radius. Cast shadows belong only to floating menus, dialogs, dragged rows, notices and tally tooltips. The toast uses an ink ground and surface text on both trees. Georgia headings, the Inter-first body stack, Charter number tokens and all board SVG paint stay fixed.

## The model

One `Workspace` tree for every viewport wider than 760px (mobile spec B8 holds). It has no tab bar, no left rail and no pane gating at any width. It is the phone's model laid side by side, layout D of the approved mock-up at https://claude.ai/artifact/Tsc7vxCgxSqRkccUhZA5tp: the board, and beside it one 440px column holding the analysis panel (Best picks, the draft recap, Best moves) or, in build mode, Board tools (D3). The site header has two rows. The brand row is the 36px brand mark (one sharp-cornered tile outline with three ranked corner dots, the best in the accent and the others in wheat and wood; `public/favicon.svg` is the same drawing) and a 31px Georgia `Unsettled` wordmark (the phone keeps its smaller pair). The controls row under it holds, left to right: the board title button, which opens Maps (D1); a spacer; the player dots pill, whose dots button opens Players (D5); the pencil, which toggles build mode; and `Import screenshot`. The title button, the pill and the pencil are the phone header's own (mobile S1), one shared component. Maps and Players are popovers hung under their buttons, 320px and 372px wide, through the native popover API anchored in CSS like `ContextMenu`: a click outside or Escape closes one and focus returns to its button. Above 980px the header and the workspace share a max width of `max(950px, calc(100vh + 334px))`, so the board reaches the window's height and the column sits against it; the grid is `minmax(494px, 1fr) 440px` and the board is sticky. Its sea is the SVG's own box with the panels' 14px corners. At 980px and below the column wraps under the board at full width and the board scrolls with the page, since Chromium bounds a sticky grid item by the whole grid and a pinned board would ride over the column. That narrow arm is the one part of this layout the mock-up does not show, so screenshot it at 900px before calling the build done. No tab strip and no footer.

Everything the phone and the desktop both render is one component with one stylesheet rule. A phone surface that reaches the desktop is promoted: its component moves out of `src/ui/phone/` into `src/ui/`, its CSS moves out of the portrait arm into the base rules, and both lose the `phone-` prefix. The portrait arm keeps only the rules that differ on a phone. The header controls (`HeaderControls`), the build block (`BoardTools`) and `buildMode.ts` are promoted with layout D. Anything still phone-only (`PhoneShell`, `PhoneHeader` as the two-row sticky frame around the shared controls, `PhoneOverlay`, the overlays, `revealBoard.ts`) keeps its name, its place and its prefix.

## Surfaces

### D1 · Library panel

Replaces the tab strip, the `Import & export` panel and the `Saved maps` panel with one panel, the desktop's version of the phone's Maps screen (S7), opened as a popover under the header's board title button. The title button shows the open board's name; this panel is the only place to switch, rename, open, delete or create a board.

- **Heading**: h2 `Maps`, and at the right a plus button (`PlusGlyph`, `aria-label="New board"`, `tab-add`) then a dots button (`DotsGlyph`, `aria-label="Import and export files"`) opening a `ContextMenu` with `Import JSON`, `Export JSON`, `Back up library` and `Restore library backup`, through `useJsonFiles`. Backup includes current unsaved games, leaving out an unlinked board still blank. Restore merges without overwriting, skips content already restored under its name or a `name (n)` copy, and refuses to evict boards at the library cap.
- **`Boards (n)`** group label with the four-key sort `MenuSelect` at its right (`SortGlyph` caret, `SORT_OPTIONS`, default `modifiedAt`), then the library warning when it applies, then the one list. Every edited board saves itself into a map, so open and saved are one thing and the list shows it once: first any open tab with no row among the maps (never saved, or linked to a map the listing cannot show: deleted in another window before the store caught up, or an unreadable library), then every saved map in sort order. The row of the board on screen is `current`, whichever kind it is; nothing else marks a map as loaded. Each row: hex (`boardColor(name)`), name, meta, trash (`TrashGlyph`, `aria-label="Delete <name>"`). A saved map's meta is the relative timestamp for the current sort key (`3 m ago`, no `LAST MODIFIED` prefix; `corrupt` for an invalid map); an unsaved board has none. The row switches to the board (`aria-label="Switch to <name>"`): `tab-select` for an unsaved board, `openMap` for a map, which selects the tab linked to it when one exists and loads the map into a new tab otherwise, silently either way unless the load raised board warnings. An invalid map's row is disabled. The name renames in place: `useRenameTab` for an unsaved board, `renameSavedMap` for a map (the library refresh retitles the linked tab). The trash on an unsaved board is `tab-close { discard: true }`; on a map it is `deleteSavedMap`, which deletes the map and, once that write has landed, closes the tab linked to it the same way. Discard is what keeps the autosave's rescue write for a closing tab from putting the board straight back as a new map, whether it was an unsaved board edited inside the debounce or a map just deleted. The library holds at most `MAX_MAPS` (50) maps: a new map beyond that evicts the least recently touched (newest of created, modified and opened), never the one just saved nor one this document still owes a write to, with a toast saying how many went, and the autosave closes the tab linked to an evicted map with discard so it cannot list as unsaved and re-save itself past the cap. The list keeps its bounded scroller with the fade masks on desktop, bounded by its own max-height so a long library never runs the popover off the page; the phone arm unbounds `.board-list` for the phone's overlay, where the one scroller is the page, never a list inside it.
- **Rows** are the phone's rows: same columns, 34px tall under a fine pointer and the phone's 44px otherwise, name bounded to its own text (mobile B6, O3). The phone Maps screen renders the very same `ListRow` component.
- **Nothing from the tab strip survives beyond select, close, rename and add.** Duplicate, Close others, Close to the right, the right-click and long-press menu, and the F2 / ⌘D / ⌘W / ⌥⌘W chords are deleted with `BoardTabs.tsx` and `shortcuts.ts`. `copyTitle` and `nextCopyName` in `boardFiles.ts` go with them.
- Landscape phones get this popover like any other workspace viewport, which gives them the document switcher they have lacked.

### D2 · Board area

- **Resting marks** (S5): while nothing is selected the board wears every listed recommendation's first pick, ranked, in the viewed player's colour, all solid. `Workspace` passes `restingMarks(board, analysis, playerId)` to `BoardCanvas` exactly as `PhoneShell` does. Marks are drawn flat everywhere: the phone's `vertex-highlight` sizing and no cast shadow become the base rule; the desktop's red defaults go.
- **Settlement order**: during the draft, each settlement carries `1` or `2` inside the piece, just above its base, in the same type as a Best picks mark's number and in ink or paper for contrast against the player's colour. Settlements are numbered per player from building array order, imported and hand-placed alike. A Best picks mark that stamps its own number on a settlement replaces the order number there. The numbers are decoration, ignore pointer events and are hidden from the accessibility tree. They disappear when the draft is complete.
- **Layout**: the board carries no layout control in either tree; the switch is the first tool row in Board tools (D3, mobile S6). The zoom chip floats in the sea's top right corner while the board is zoomed and takes no row of its own, so the board gets that height. The `19/19 terrain` and `4 pieces` counts leave the caption. `BoardCanvas` no longer consults `usePhone`.
- The desktop board stage has no background, border, shadow or padding, and the canvas has no drop shadow. The sea is the stage. Above 980px the canvas is capped at `calc(100vh - 122px)`, reserving the shell padding and both header rows within the viewport. The 980px arm keeps `max(320px, calc(100dvh - 130px))`. The phone sea frame uses a 14px radius without a shadow.

### D3 · Board tools

- The pencil opens Board tools in the column in place of the analysis panel (build mode), on both trees. It snapshots the game on entry: `Done` keeps the edits, `Cancel` restores the snapshot, and both leave build mode (mobile B1), from a footer strip at the bottom of the panel. Entering build mode arms the default taps below with no tool button selected; leaving it clears the tool, and `Shell` drops the tool when the viewport swaps trees. There is no `Edit manually` fold. Outside build mode and with no board pick active, the board shows no placement dots and a tap changes it only through the Best picks circles, by the draft placement rule in D4.
- The heading is `Board tools`, with borderless `Undo` and `Redo` icon buttons (`panel-dots`) on the right, drawn as the straight arrows (`ArrowLeftGlyph` / `ArrowRightGlyph`) like the Best moves heading. They are the store's undo and redo, each disabled while its stack is empty. Nothing else sits in the heading on either tree. Under it a muted help paragraph reads: `Tap the board to edit pieces, the robber and ports. Pick a terrain or number, then tap a tile. A picked terrain turns a tapped port 2:1.` It uses full stops and commas, never semicolons, and stays a short paragraph: the owner tried it as a labelled list on 2026-10-13 and found it long and no more helpful, and on 2026-11-12 asked for it cut to this length.
- **Layout** is the first tool row: two buttons, `4 players` and `5–6 players`, with the board's own layout drawn selected. Choosing the other one switches the layout, asking first with the `Change layout?` dialog when the board is not blank. The confirmed switch also resets the game record, as the clears do, and its dialog reads `Switching layouts clears the tiles, tokens, and pieces on this board and resets recorded turns, hands, development cards and awards. Your players are kept.` A blank board switches straight away and keeps its record.
- **`Import screenshot`**: the filled accent hero button (`ImportButton`), 34px tall, last in the header's controls row; opens `ImportDialog`. The phone keeps the same button at the top of its Maps screen (S7).
- The screenshot dialog keeps keyboard focus inside, closes on Escape and restores the opener's focus. Imported players are named by colour, and the import claims Settled's highlighted pill (the player to move) or Colonist's viewer. Names can be edited in Players. When the screenshot's layout, tiles, number tokens and ports all equal a library map's, or those of an open board that is in no map yet (an import is not saved until its first edit), the dialog swaps the drop zone for one question, `Import as new` or `Update`. `Update` opens that board and commits the screenshot into it as one undo step: roster order, pieces, robber and hand sizes come from the screenshot, while players matched by colour keep their saved id, name, claim, dev cards, knights, VP cards, awards and turn (`mergeScreenshot` in `model/game.ts`). What the screenshot did not read stays as saved: a hand whose count was unreadable, the robber when none was found, the whole roster and its order when the screenshot had no roster, the seat order when it showed no turn order, and the building order when it showed no pick order.
- **Terrain** and **Number token** each carry a count on their label, right-aligned: tiles placed over hexes (`12/19`), and number tokens placed over the hexes that are not a desert (`11/18`; a blank hex counts, since it will need one). A count short of its total is the danger red at weight 700; a full one is muted. With a terrain selected a tap paints the tile, and a tap on a tile that already has that terrain blanks it, taking its robber with it as the old erase tool did. With a number selected a tap sets the token, and a tap on a tile that already has that number removes it. Selecting the selected tool again deselects it.
- **Board** is the last row, three equal buttons with a glyph over a sentence-case label: `Randomize` (`DiceGlyph`), `Clear pieces` and `Clear board`, the two clears in the danger red. None is a selected tool. `Randomize` deals a random board. On a board that is not blank (`isBlank`, the layout switch's rule) it first asks `Randomize this board?` through `ConfirmDialog`: `Replace` (danger) deals the random map onto this board and resets the game record, as the clears do (owner's choice 2026-10-13: it removes every piece, and otherwise the stale record could only be reset by `Clear board`), `New board` (primary) leaves this board alone and opens a new one with the same layout and players and a random map, named like any new board by `newBoardTitle`, and `Cancel`, which has the focus, does nothing. Its message reads `Replace swaps this board's tiles, tokens, ports and pieces for a random map and resets recorded turns, hands, development cards and awards. New board keeps this one and deals the random map on a new board. Your players are kept either way.` A blank board is dealt straight away and keeps its record, and so is a board that is still the very map randomize last dealt in this session; any edit, or reopening the app, makes it a board to ask about. `Clear pieces` removes every building and road in one commit and leaves tiles, tokens, ports and the robber; it is disabled while the board has no building and no road. `Clear board` is `clearBoard` in one commit and is disabled on a blank board. Both clears also reset the game record (the owner chose this over keeping hands and cards, 2026-10-13), since it describes a game the board no longer holds: the recorded turn, the typed dev hand, every player's hand, unseen cards, dev cards, knights and VP cards, and both award holders. Players and their order stay. Each asks first. `Clear the pieces?` reads `This removes every settlement, city and road. The game goes back to before the draft: recorded turns, hands, development cards and awards are reset. The tiles, numbers, ports and robber stay, and so do your players. Undo brings everything back.` with `Clear pieces` (danger) and `Cancel`. `Clear this board?` reads `This removes every tile and number, every settlement, city and road, and the robber, and puts the ports back where a new board has them. The game goes back to before the draft: recorded turns, hands, development cards and awards are reset. Your players stay. Undo brings everything back.` with `Clear board` (danger) and `Cancel`. `useBoardActions` holds all four questions for both trees, tied to the board each was asked about, so one closes if another window deletes that board.
- **Default taps.** There is no robber, port or erase tool; the `Tool` kinds are `none`, `tile`, `token` and `piece`. In build mode with no tool button selected, a board click acts on the nearest target within half a hex edge (29 board units). The targets, drawn for the owner as the `Tap zones` view of the mock-up: a legal corner or an existing building (cycles empty, settlement, city, empty); a legal edge or an existing road (toggles); the centre of any tiled hex other than the robber's own (the robber moves there); an existing port badge (removes the port); and the badge position of an empty coastal edge neither of whose corners touches a port (adds a 3:1 port there). That last rule keeps a new-port spot from sitting beside a port's badge, where the two split one tap zone. A click within 10 board units of the previous click stays on that click's target, or does nothing if that target is no longer actionable; a click on another target's dot, or within 8 units of another target, acts on that target instead. While the draft is in progress, a settlement cycles straight to empty. A tapped piece keeps its owner, and the brush player colours only new pieces. Super city joins the cycle after city only on a board that already has one; a super city always cycles to empty. With no brush player, existing pieces, ports, empty-coast spots and tiles stay actionable. Legal corner and edge dots show together as small solid dots in the placement player's colour with a contrasting rim, and an empty-coast spot shows a neutral dot. A fine pointer previews a see-through settlement or road at an empty target and hides that target's dot; an existing piece keeps the unfilled ring in its owner's colour, and a port keeps a neutral ring. A tile centre has no dot and no preview. Board picks use the same reach over their legal corners or edges.
- Neither tree has a board dots menu, so `ContextMenu` loses its `history` pair. `ContextMenuItem.count` is deleted.

### D4 · Analysis panel

One code path for every pointer; the `coarse` branch and the `variant` prop go.

Placement analysis runs in a shared worker. A changed board or viewed pick shows a thin progress bar until its matching result arrives, with no predicted marks. The bar runs the full width of the panel's top edge, clipped by its rounded corners, and fills with the engine's own progress reports (`onProgress`, sent by the worker a twentieth at a time): each ranking the job runs, nested or its own, takes a share sized by an estimate of its cost, and a nested ranking the worker already holds takes none. The last ranking stays in place under the bar as inert placeholders (its text drawn as blank bars and its hex clusters as flat silhouettes), so the panel keeps its height; a result already cached shows at once with no bar. The bar and the blanking wait 120ms, so a result that returns at once changes nothing on screen. Only a body that was on screen is held: after a first load, the empty board, the claim row or an error, `LISTED_PICKS` generic placeholder rows stand in, and only where a ranking can follow (the board has a number token and the viewed player has a pick left); otherwise the bar shows alone. A failed view that is returned to reads as pending while it is analysed again. The status text `Analyzing placements…` is kept for screen readers only. A failed worker offers `Retry analysis`. Board editing and navigation remain available.

- **Selecting a row** (click or tap) pins its marks in the viewed player's colour, first pick and its road stub solid, planned second pick and its road stub faded, with no stub for a pick that opens nothing, each circle numbered as that player's first or second settlement (a row ranking their second settlement shows `2`), and opens `Place settlement` / `Clear` under it; clicking the selected row again deselects. **Hovering** a row with a fine pointer previews its marks only while nothing is selected; leaving the list restores the resting marks. The likely-gone block behaves the same way with `Play these out` / `Clear`. A row click never places a piece directly. During an unfinished draft on a board with tiles, outside build mode, with no board pick active and an open pick viewed, a board tap within half a hex edge (29 board units) of a first-pick circle (a resting ranked circle, or the selected or hovered row's solid circle) places the viewed player's settlement on that circle's corner and moves the view to the earliest unplaced pick. The settlement comes with the setup road a ranking of that seat would draw for it on the board as it stands. It places only that corner, leaving the likely-gone picks unplaced. No other tap changes the board: the faded planned second-pick circle, a likely-gone circle, a contest ring, an unmarked legal corner and an existing piece all do nothing, and no legal-corner dots are drawn. Until the draft is finished, every road and building on the board is drawn at 70% opacity so the Best picks circles lead. A hovered or selected row also rings each contested expansion spot one road beyond its setup road. A rival contests a spot when a placed road ends there or ends one road short across an edge that is free or theirs. Each ring uses the first contesting rival's colour in roster order, with a dark outer edge and radius 16, or 19 around a planned second-pick circle. Rings read placed pieces only, never the likely-gone line, and change no score, ranking or road.
- **Heading** `Best picks`, with borderless `Step back one pick` and `Step forward one pick` icon buttons (`panel-dots`, as in the other panel headings) on the right whenever the board has tiles, including an unclaimed draft. The heading is drawn like Best moves: the title alone on the left, and the step buttons are the straight arrows (`ArrowLeftGlyph` / `ArrowRightGlyph`). A finished draft shows the draft recap and then Best moves (D8) instead, whose headings hold `Undo` and `Redo`. Back removes the last snake picker's last settlement and its touching roads owned by that player, falling back to the last settlement in board order when that picker has none. It is disabled with no settlements, any upgraded building, or more settlements than draft picks. Forward restores the removed pick and roads. Each step is an ordinary board edit; the local forward list survives player claims while the building and road arrays stay identical, and is discarded by a piece edit, a tab switch, or store undo/redo. Forward is disabled when that list is empty or invalid. **Context line** `<picker> · Picking 3rd and 6th` when viewing that player's first remaining pick, or `<picker> · Picking 6th` for a later one. The name menu, labelled `Whose picks to show`, views the selected player's first unplaced pick without changing the claim. It lists players with picks remaining plus the viewed player.
- **Draft ribbon**: under the heading whenever the board has tiles, centred with space above the centred context line or the claim row. The circles are drawn like the Best moves turn order: a 1.5px ink border that fades with a later pick's fill, an Inter 800 numeral and the same ring. They are 30px across while the row fits and shrink together, numerals and ring included, when it does not (`--slots` on the ribbon, container query units on the slot). Each numbered circle views that pick without changing the claim; if nobody is claimed, it claims the circle's player first. A placed circle views that player's first unplaced pick, or does nothing if they have none. Its title and accessible name read `Pick 3: Red`, and `aria-pressed` is true on every circle of the viewed player. Taken picks and the current pick are solid and later picks faded. The ink ring marks the viewed pick, defaulting to the claimed player's next unplaced pick, and is absent while nobody is claimed; ribbon slots come from draft inference without analysis predictions. The viewed pick is temporary UI state, outside persistence and undo history. Changing the claim or switching boards resets the view. When viewing the earliest open pick, the view follows the draft through board edits, undo, redo and the Best picks step buttons; `Place settlement` on that pick moves the view to the next open pick. A later pick viewed on purpose stays viewed while its slot remains unplaced and is not the claimed player's next pick.
- **Viewing a later pick**: recommendations rank that slot after every preceding unplaced pick, including the viewed player's earlier pick. `Likely gone before this pick` lists those picks in draft order as `{n}. {name} {spot} {pct}%`, with the spot in a small hex cluster and entries wrapping without separators, including the viewed player under their own name. The likely-gone marks include each picker's road stub in that player's colour. `Play these out` lays those settlements and their setup roads in one commit. `Place settlement` lays the same line plus the selected first settlement and its road for the viewed player in one commit. Neither action places the planned second pick. Both leave the claim unchanged. A last pick has no planned follow-up. The planned second a row draws is read off the same rollout as the likely-gone line whenever the row's pick survives that line, so it is not a corner the line takes; the row's other planned seconds follow by how often they were planned. Changing the viewed pick clears selected rows and marks; clicking the same view leaves them alone.
- **No player claimed**: the hint `Tap your colour and the ranking starts` (desktop: `Pick your colour and the ranking starts`) and a row of swatch buttons, one per player, that claim on click. The `choose player` placeholder goes.
- **Empty board** (mobile B5): heading `This board is empty` and the hint `Import a screenshot to read the tiles, numbers and players off it, or place them yourself.` The `Import screenshot` and `Build it by hand` buttons follow on both trees; `Build it by hand` enters build mode.
- **Ranked rows** form one ledger with hairlines between rows. A 20px rank circle keeps the viewed player's colour and ink ring; each spot is one inline SVG of the corner's hexes in their board colours and positions, with token discs on numbered hexes (including blank hexes), a desert cactus and any port badge in its sea position. The pick sits in a 48.5px thumbnail column and uses 14px hex sides; follow-ups and Likely gone use 11.5px sides. Availability and wrapping `then` / `or` follow-up clusters sit beside the thumbnail, followed by the score column. The score stays in ink and availability text uses `--accent-dark`; hovering an unselected row changes only its score to `--accent-dark`. Selection adds a 1px accent border, 6px radius and 7% accent wash without a ring. Factors open with selection as muted text with semibold ink values. Loading clusters become flat tint silhouettes; generic placeholders use the same ledger shape, 74px per row including its hairline, with a rank disc, thumbnail block, body bar and score block. Every listed row with a contest shows a muted 11px note on its own line below the first-pick tiles and row body, after the score and before selected factors, spanning columns 2 to the end. Rivals with the same count share a clause: `{Names} can reach {k} of {m} spots`, or `{Names} can reach the only spot` for one spot. Names and groups follow roster order, names use commas and a final `and`, and clauses join with `; `. The note wraps without clipping and stays with its ranking's board and viewed seat while that ranking is held as placeholders.

### D5 · Players panel

The roster is the phone's roster (S8) plus the tally the desktop keeps (mobile O1). It opens as a popover under the dots button of the header's player pill.

Below the roster, `Award holders` opens Longest road and Largest army selectors when anyone qualifies. Only tied leaders can be chosen. Imported ties start as `Unknown`, while a recorded incumbent keeps a tie. Corrections share game undo, redo and persistence.

- **Heading**: h2 `Players`, and at the right a plus button (`PlusGlyph`, `aria-label="Add player"`), disabled at six players.
- **Row**, left to right: grip (`GripGlyph`, starts a drag at once), swatch (24px, the player's colour), name cell, the tally columns and VP under the tally bar. The bar's view switch is two text tabs at its left end, `Pieces` and `Resources`, the current one in ink with an accent underline sitting on the bar's rule; the column icons are all 14px. The `YOU` chip sits immediately after the name inside the name cell. Only the claimed row draws the chip. An award holder has no badge: the road or knight count that won the card is bold.
- The name cell is the row's only flexing cell and holds both the name and, on the claimed row, the `YOU` chip. The name clips to leave the chip visible. The popover is 372px wide, so the tally columns, the row gaps and the tab spacing take their full size. The tabs and each row's grip dots start at the panel's content edge, under the `Players` title.
- **Clicking the row claims** that player (`setMe`); the claimed row is `me`, with no tint and a transparent 1px border. The claim is the phone's row-wide `list-row-select` button rather than a handler on the row, so it is reachable by keyboard and carries the claimed state (`aria-pressed`); the grip, swatch, name, tally and VP are positioned to paint over it and keep their own clicks and tooltips. **Clicking the swatch toggles the piece brush**, as a header dot does in build mode (mobile S1), (`active-player`), carried by `aria-pressed` with no ring on the swatch: the brush player colours only new pieces, and the brush does not tint the row. The roster has no hand or dev card steppers: the Best moves table's row editor (D8) is the one place to correct them.
- **Clicking the name renames in place** (`InlineRename`, dotted underline at rest in the phone shell and on a coarse pointer, and only on hover or keyboard focus with a fine pointer, the field bounded to the text, Enter commits, Escape reverts, `renamePlayer` on commit rather than on every keystroke).
- **Dragging**: the rows the lifted row passes ease out of the way (`rowShift`) and the mid-drag trash row removes. The lifted row has an opaque surface ground and the shared floating shadow. Fine pointers keep `useRowReorder`'s HTML5 path, where the browser's own drag image follows the pointer and the lifted row instead eases into the slot it would land in, so the list previews the order it will commit to; on a hold the lifted row tracks the finger with no easing. Every offset derives from `dragId` and `dropTarget`, which both paths supply.
- The native drag is aimed and dropped on the **list**, not on the rows: a row under the pointer has already moved out from under it, and the gap a row leaves behind is bare list, which would refuse the drop. Both paths therefore resolve the destination with `dropIndexFor` against the layout measured when the drag started.
- **Snake draft**: the ribbon lives in Best picks (D4). The named grid and its `DraftLabel` stay on the phone's Players screen; the desktop roster already names every seat.

### D6 · Vocabulary

### D8 · Best moves

Once every starting settlement is placed the analysis panel first shows the draft recap, in both trees from one component: each player's chance of winning from the drafted position, a header with one player's win chance and their lead or deficit, a share bar, one card per player with their two spots and percentage, and a `Start game` button. The recap shows only while the game has no recorded turn, every building is a settlement and every player owns exactly two; `Start game` records the first player's turn, and undo brings the recap back. How the chance is measured and the full surface are in the adviser spec under "Draft recap". From the first recorded turn the panel is Best moves, in both trees from one component. It tracks the game turn by turn for every player and advises whoever's turn it is. The behaviour behind it (what the advice reads, what each recorded action does to hands) is in [`.claude/specs/adviser/spec.md`](../adviser/spec.md); this section is the surface. The first approved mock-up was https://claude.ai/artifact/ChDoUgMJR5yt7VYgDWJbLo; the record-your-turn redesign below replaced its action bar.

Two sections, each its own panel, in both trees. The approved mock-up of this surface (direction C with B's action tiles) is https://claude.ai/artifact/FXTAEV92eXLhZYSo7qsRSs, and its decisions are recorded in [`.claude/specs/best-moves-record/plan.md`](../best-moves-record/plan.md).

**Record your turn** (`Record Red's turn` for a rival), top to bottom:

- **Heading**: the title, with borderless `Undo` and `Redo` buttons on the right. They are the store's undo and redo, drawn as straight arrows (`ArrowLeftGlyph` / `ArrowRightGlyph`). Stepping a draft pick back is an undo, or a piece tapped off in build mode.
- **Turn order** replaces the draft ribbon: one numbered circle per player in seat order, tinted to the player, the active player ringed. A circle is a button named `Turn 2: Red`; clicking it makes it that player's turn, so the advice and the rail are theirs. Nothing marks the claimed player here.
- **Context line**, centred: the active player's name and what the last recorded action did (`Rolled 8 · aki +2 wheat +1 ore · Red +1 wheat`), or `not rolled yet`.
- **Table**: one row per player, the active row tinted. A two-way switch picks the view. `Cards`: the five resources, the hand total and the dev card count; a resource is a number when known and a range (`1–3`) while the player holds cards nobody saw. `Points`: settlements, cities, road length, knights, VP cards and the total, with a star on each award holder. Under the table, `Your dev cards` lists the claimed player's cards by type and their VP cards, as chips in three plainly different states: a playable card is ink on white with an ink ring, a kind you hold none of is muted on the paper tint, and a card bought this turn is a faded grey outline with no fill and no label until it can be played. Clicking a row opens stepper chips under the table to correct that player by hand: resources, unseen cards and dev cards, and for the claimed player the dev cards by type.
- **Step rail**: three numbered 26px circles joined by a hairline. A finished step is a bright green disc with a white tick, the current step has a 2.5px ink border, a later step a faint border and muted text.
  1. **Roll**: eleven totals in one row, each with its pip dots under the number, seven outlined in the accent with an empty pip row so it lines up. A total commits at once, production included. The step then folds to `Rolled 8` with an icon-only pencil `Change roll` button on the right, and the production summary on its own line under it (each producing player as a swatch, the name and one resource icon per card; `Nobody produces` when nobody does). `Change roll` shows for the whole turn: it commits the game as it was just before the roll, striking the roll and every action recorded after it from the log, and one Undo brings them all back. It is hidden when that pre-roll state has left the undo history (after a reload). The log and the production summary are kept per committed game, so they survive a remount, Board tools and undoing End turn, but not a reload. On a seven every player holding more than seven cards gets a discard line under the folded step (the advised cards for the claimed player; `discards 4, types not seen` with an `Enter types` alternative for a rival, or `Skip`). The robber prompt and the blinking tokens follow only once every discard line is recorded or skipped. Whether the seven's robber has moved is `turn.robberMoved` in the game, so it survives a reload.
  2. **Actions**: a log of this turn's recorded actions (undo removes an entry), and action buttons, each shown only when it can happen: `Trade`, `Buy card`, `Play card` and `Build`. `Trade` needs cards in hand. `Buy card` needs a hand that could afford one (the known shortfall is no more than the unseen cards) and a deck not known to be empty (the last count the adviser reported for this tab). `Play card` needs a playable card: a typed one for the claimed player, otherwise a public dev card not bought this turn (`turn.devBought`), and never a second card in one turn. `Build` needs a piece that is affordable, has a legal spot and has supply left. No tile shows while a seven's robber is pending. After the roll with no tile available, the step shows done as `No actions possible`, unless a tray is open. They are chips (glyph left of a medium-weight label, sized to the text) in one row from the left. Where the row would wrap (four chips need about 356px of step width, three at most 279px) a container query on the step body turns them into equal tiles, glyph over label, so the actions never wrap: on a 390px phone four show as tiles and three as chips. A button opens its controls under the row; a selected one has an accent border over a light accent fill. Undo and redo close an open tray.
     - `Trade`, party last: five resource buttons for what the player gives and five for what they get, each tap adding one card, and `Clear`. Then `With`: the bank at the player's best rate for the one resource given (`Bank 4:1`, `Port 3:1`, `Port 2:1`, recording rate times the cards received), or another player, recording the counted cards. Neither records a resource that is in both rows. The tap on the party records the trade. When the claimed player is the one answering a one-for-one style offer, `Advice for you: Accept` or `Decline` shows with the margin and the reason.
     - `Buy card`: a rival's purchase records on the tile tap; the claimed player picks the card drawn, which records it.
     - `Play card`: the card (only the typed playable kinds for the claimed player), then its own inputs: the robber move for a Knight, two road spots for Road Building (the second records it), two resource taps for Year of Plenty (the second records it, the same resource twice is allowed, and `Clear` drops the first), and for Monopoly the resource plus how many each other player gave, prefilled with what is known and capped at what they could hold.
     - `Build`: the buildable pieces among road, settlement and city with their cost, then a click on the spot on the board.
  3. **End turn**: one full-width primary button; it commits at once and moves the ring to the next seat.
- **Robber**, on a seven or a Knight: every number token except the robber's pulses (a static accent ring under reduced motion) and a tap on a hex moves the robber there; the robber's own hex ignores taps. For the claimed player the advised hex is ringed and a line names it, the victim and the reason. A hex with no rival building that holds cards records at once; otherwise a popover at the hex lists those players with their card counts and `Nobody`, and a tap on a name records it, unless the claimed player is the robber or the victim, when a five-resource row asks what was stolen and that tap records it.

**Best moves**, labelled `Your best moves` / `Best moves for Red`:

- **Recommendations**. Before the roll, one dashed card titled `Before you roll` holds the dev card to play or `Hold`. After it, ranked cards have a surface ground, hairline border and 6px radius, with an ink border when selected. Each shows a rank circle in the active player's colour, the action, the policy's raw score, a one-line reason, and the cost as flat tinted chips while selected. Selecting a card pins its mark on the board under the Best picks select, pin and hover rules and opens `Apply` / `Clear`. `Apply` commits when the advice is the whole fact (a build, a bank trade, a discard, Year of Plenty); `Pass` reads `End turn`; otherwise it opens the matching action with the advice filled in (`Apply: who accepted?`, the `Drew` picker, the robber flow). During a seven the list is replaced by a hint to record the discards and the robber first.
- **Notes**, small and muted: `Bank estimated: 6 cards of unknown type`, `2 of Red's cards are of unknown type and ignored`, a rival's dev cards treated as unplayed knights. A note appears only when it applies.

Every recorded action is one commit, so one undo takes back the piece and the cards together. Advice comes from a worker and never runs during render; while a changed game is pending the old cards disappear. A robber destination is marked by a dashed ring on its hex, the one board mark this panel adds.

The Players panel shows the same game read-only: the table's row editor is the only place hands, dev cards, knights and VP cards are edited.

## Decisions

- **The board title lives in the header.** The title button names the open board and opens Maps, which is the only place to rename it.
- **Tab strip actions match the phone.** Only select, close, rename and new survive; the context menu, duplicate, close-others, close-to-the-right and every chord are deleted, not relocated.
- **The phone's model, side by side (2026-10-13).** The owner found the phone more pleasant than the desktop: it shows the board and one block, where the desktop showed five panels, put Best picks last under Players and left its left rail mostly empty. So the left rail went, Maps and Players open from the header, and Board tools replace the analysis panel behind the pencil. Best picks keeps its panel rather than sitting on the page ground as it does on the phone.
- **Ports by tap, no port editor (2026-11-12).** The `Edit port` dialog is gone. With no tool selected a port badge removes the port and an empty-coast spot adds a 3:1 port (D3 default taps). With a resource terrain selected, port badges and empty-coast spots are targets too and show the same neutral dots: a tap on a port that is already 2:1 for that resource makes it 3:1 for any resource, any other port becomes 2:1 for that resource, and an empty-coast spot gets a 2:1 port for that resource. A tap that lands on a hex stays a tile edit. The desert terrain and the number tools leave ports alone. A port of any other rate arrives only from an import.
- **No robber, port or erase tool (2026-10-13).** Taps on the board do those jobs directly, and the two whole-board resets the owner wanted (pieces only, and everything) sit with randomize in the `Board` row.
- **Landscape phones are plain workspace viewports.** They scroll the page like a short desktop window; nothing is gated behind a pane.
- **The empty-board buttons show on both trees.** Both have build mode now, so `Build it by hand` means the same thing everywhere.
- **Desktop keeps hover previews**, layered under the phone's select-on-click, rather than dropping hover.
