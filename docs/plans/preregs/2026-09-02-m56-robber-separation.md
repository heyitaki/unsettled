# M-56 preregistration: blockability within hex-count strata

Date: 2026-09-02. Committed before the run.

**What this measures, and the question it answers.** M-48 found that pairs piling a larger share of their pips onto a single hex win 11.37pp less often between the outer quartiles, negative at every draft slot, and that reading is what brought the dropped robber-attraction term back as an open item inside SP3's scope. M-48 also recorded, in the same entry, the reason the term is not yet specifiable: blockability and producing-hex count are mechanically entangled. The share sitting on a pair's top hex has a floor near one over the number of distinct producing hexes the pair touches, so the concentrated quartile is enriched in low-hex-count pairs, and M-47 found low-hex-count picks win about 5pp less often. Nothing in SP0 separated "one robber can take this economy away" from "this pair touches few hexes". This run performs that separation and nothing else: it reads the same blockability quartile gap *within* strata of fixed producing-hex count, where hex count cannot vary and therefore cannot be the thing the gap is measuring.

**The statistic.** Every completed pair already carries `expansion.rs::PairReading::producing_hexes`, the count of distinct hexes carrying both a resource and a token adjacent to either settlement. `expansion.rs::blockability_by_hex_count` groups the pairs by that count and runs the *identical* `expansion.rs::quartile_gap` construction inside each group: quarter cuts on values rather than ranks, top arm's win rate minus bottom arm's. Each stratum reports its hex count, its pair count, whether it is counted, and its `QuartileGap`. Beside the strata the table reports `countedPairs`, the pair-count-weighted mean gap over the counted strata, and the boolean `concentrationTermIndicated`. A stratum is counted when it holds at least 1000 pairs, the constant `expansion.rs::CONCENTRATION_STRATUM_PAIRS`. The table is emitted on the overall row and on each of the four per-slot rows of `diagnostics.json`; the boolean is read off the overall row, exactly as `robberAttractionRevisit` is, and the per-slot booleans are record-only.

**Why the gap is read within strata rather than adjusted for hex count.** Stratification holds the confound exactly rather than approximately, and it needs no model of how win rate depends on hex count. A regression adjustment would need that model, and M-47's 5pp is a contrast between two self-selected groups of pip-matched picks rather than a population slope, so there is no honest coefficient to adjust by. Stratifying also keeps the reading constructed the same way as M-48's, which is the only way the two magnitudes may be compared at all; a second gap built differently would tell us nothing about the first. The cost is stated now rather than discovered later: unlike M-48's per-slot rows, whose cuts were identical at all four slots, the per-stratum cuts will differ by stratum, because a pair on four hexes cannot reach the low shares a pair on six hexes can. That is the confound working as described, and it means the strata's arms are not interchangeable across strata; only the gaps are compared, and only through the weighted mean.

**Passing condition (fixed before the run, and already in the code).** The concentration term is built if and only if **both** halves hold on the overall row:

1. the pair-count-weighted mean gap over counted strata is at most `-0.03`, the constant `expansion.rs::CONCENTRATION_MEAN_GAP`; **and**
2. every counted stratum's gap is strictly negative.

Both, never either. **The mean guards magnitude:** a term is worth building only if concentration still costs games at a size worth pricing once hex count is held fixed, and 3 percentage points is roughly a quarter of the pooled `-11.37pp` M-48 read, which is the point below which the pooled reading would be better explained as the confound than as the effect. **The every-stratum sign guards against one stratum carrying the whole reading:** a weighted mean is a weighted mean, so a single large stratum with a strong negative gap can drag it past the threshold while the other strata point the other way, and a term justified by that mean would be a term about one hex count wearing the name of a general effect. Requiring the sign in every counted stratum makes the claim the term rests on the claim the term actually needs: that concentration costs games at every hex count the run observed in quantity, not on average across them.

Two edges of that condition, both already implemented and both stated here so they cannot be read as a later choice. A stratum below 1000 pairs enters neither the mean nor the sign test, so the boolean is read over the bulk of the mass and pairs in thin strata are invisible to it; a thin stratum's gap is still reported and is record-only. A stratum whose two quarter cuts coincide is degenerate, contributes 0 to the mean and, being not strictly negative, fails the sign test rather than passing it on an artifact, which is the same carve-out `robberAttractionRevisit` already uses and which the programme's SP0-D2 bullet states. If no stratum is counted at all, `countedPairs` is 0 and the boolean is false.

**Design.** The same single `diagnose` invocation as M-47 and M-48, whose design those preregistrations record: `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 4000 games and 16,000 completed pairs, every seat on `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with `--player-trading`, placement `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json`, `--alpha 0.05`, `--threads 0`. Only `--out` differs. The invocation is repeated rather than the M-48 artifact being re-read, because the stratified table did not exist when that run executed and `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so there is no artifact to re-read.

**Command.** Build first, from the repo root, so no compile lands inside a timed window:

```text
RUSTFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo build --release --manifest-path simulator/Cargo.toml -p unsettled-sim
```

Then, from `simulator/`, with `uptime` recorded immediately before and immediately after:

```text
target/release/unsettled-sim diagnose --layout standard4 --seats 4 --domain tuning --placement app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --boards 2000 --reps 2 --policy heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial --player-trading --alpha 0.05 --threads 0 --out runs/m56-robber-separation
```

**This is a diagnostic, not an A/B.** No arms, no reference, no field contrast, no hero-seat rotation, so it returns no verdict, and the programme's paired-statistics disposition rule (`better` keeps, `worse` drops, `equivalent` at power drops, `inconclusive` buys units once) does not apply to it and cannot be read onto it. What it produces is one boolean, `concentrationTermIndicated`, which decides whether Tasks 14, 15 and 16 of `docs/plans/simulator-placement-sp3-sp5.md` run or take their skip branch. Nothing here adopts or changes a shipped default in either direction, and nothing here builds the term: the term is Task 14's business and only on the true branch.

**What the strata gaps carry, and what they do not.** Point contrasts with their arm sizes, means and cut values, and no interval and no significance claim, exactly as M-48's two gaps carried none. `--alpha 0.05` rides on the run for the coastal-selection block's clustered interval and touches nothing this reading uses. The condition is a comparison of magnitudes and signs against fixed constants rather than a test against a null, which is a deliberate limitation of a sizing reading and is stated here so the M entry cannot later be cited as though the per-stratum gaps carried significance. The sign test in particular is being applied to point estimates, so a counted stratum whose true gap is near zero can fail it on noise; that asymmetry is intended, because the branch it guards buys a new term and the conservative direction is not to build one.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** I predict **`concentrationTermIndicated` comes back false**, on the magnitude half rather than the sign half, with a weighted mean gap between `-1pp` and `-3pp` and most likely inside the threshold. The reasoning is the confound's size. M-48's blockability cuts were `<= 0.2380952` and `>= 0.2777778`. A pair spreading its pips evenly over six producing hexes sits near 0.167 and over four near 0.25, so those cuts do not split concentrated pairs from spread ones at a fixed hex count; they split five-and-six-hex pairs from three-and-four-hex pairs almost directly. If the two outer quartiles differ by roughly two hexes on average and each hex is worth even 3pp, the confound alone accounts for most of the pooled `-11.37pp`, which leaves little for concentration itself to explain. Working the other way, within a stratum a high top-hex share means the pair holds a fat 6 or 8 with a thin remainder, and the fat token is worth real production, so the within-stratum contrast is closer to two-sided than the pooled one was. I expect the strata to run from about 3 to 6 producing hexes with three or four of them clearing the 1000-pair floor, and I expect the counted gaps to be small and mostly negative.

I would not be surprised to be wrong, and the shape of being wrong matters more than the call. **The reading that would change my mind is a weighted mean at or past `-5pp` with every counted stratum negative**, which would say concentration costs games at every hex count and the pooled `-11.37pp` was mostly real. **The most likely way the boolean fails despite a large mean is the sign test**, through one thin-but-counted stratum at an extreme hex count reading positive; if that happens the M entry records the mean and the offending stratum side by side, and the term still is not built, because the condition is a conjunction and this preregistration does not get to relax it after seeing which half broke. M-54's prediction post-mortem is the reason for the hedging here: the lesson recorded there is that a term with a new input is far more active than reasoning from correlation with existing terms suggests, and the same caution applies to reasoning about how much of a pooled gap a confound is carrying.

**Admissibility.** The release binary is built before the measurement starts and the build is confirmed to report `Finished` against a warm fingerprint, which is M-50's lesson. `uptime` is recorded immediately before and immediately after the single `diagnose` invocation and both readings enter the M entry. Zero illegal actions required. The invocation shares its command block with no build, no test suite and no other CPU-heavy job. One run of 4000 games, expected in well under the 5-minute ceiling on M-47's observed 0.78s at 18 workers. `diagnostics.json` is deterministic and carries no elapsed time or worker count, so a repeat at any `--threads` is byte-identical and the artifact itself is not evidence of load; `uptime` is. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so the artifact stays out of the tree and the M entry is the record.

**One extra admissibility check this run can make that M-48 could not.** Everything the run replays is unchanged since M-48: the same board set, the same seeds, the same policy, and a placement whose weights vector moved only by gaining `expansionWeight` and `expansionDecay`, which ship at 0 and 0.5 with the walk skipped at a weight of 0, verified byte-identical through the corpus recapture SP3's tasks ran. The stratified table is strictly additive over the existing statistics. **The overall boxing gap must therefore reproduce M-48's `+0.0646413` and the overall blockability gap its `-0.1137321` exactly, and the coastal-selection block must reproduce M-47's 6,049 pairs and `0.4518102` share.** If any of those does not reproduce, the run is inadmissible, no reading is taken off it and no branch is decided; the discrepancy is investigated first, because a moved number means something in the field moved that was declared not to have. This check is cheap and it is the strongest evidence available that SP3's behaviour-neutral tasks were in fact behaviour-neutral.
