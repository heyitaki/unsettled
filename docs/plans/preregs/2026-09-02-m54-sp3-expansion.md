# M-54 preregistration: the SP3 expansion term

Date: 2026-09-02. Committed before the run.

**Change under test.** SP3 added an `expansion` component to the placement formula, closing the arithmetic half of `SIM-GAP-34`: the formula prices what a candidate produces and nothing about what it opens, so a settlement hemmed in by rival pieces scores exactly like an identical one with an open fan of second-ring sites. The term walks outward from the candidate over the current occupancy, collects the sites the candidate would open, values each one by the existing formula, and pays for the best two. A site is a vertex at path length at least 2 from the candidate that would be legal once the candidate is placed (unoccupied, no occupied neighbour, not adjacent to the candidate), reachable within two *paid* road builds, where the seat's own roads cost nothing to travel, the first unowned edge on a path is free because it is the setup road, and a rival's road is impassable while a vertex a rival holds is a dead end. A site's value is `marginalTotal` of that site given the seat's holdings plus the candidate, with `receivesGrant` false and `expansionWeight` forced to 0 so the term cannot recurse, discounted by `expansionDecay ^ paidBuilds`. The term is `expansionWeight * (sum of the two largest discounted site values)`, computed by `src/engine/expansion.ts::expansionTerm` and its mirror `simulator/crates/engine/src/placement/expansion.rs`, pinned against each other by parity class `W13`. It ships at `expansionWeight` 0, where both scorers skip the walk entirely and every committed vector's numbers are bit-identical to the pre-SP3 tree, which the corpus diff in that task confirmed. `expansionDecay` ships at 0.5 and is inert while the weight is 0. This run asks the only question that can move the weight off 0: does paying for the sites a settlement opens win more games?

**The arms.** Two decision arms on one axis, both the live `simulator/placement/default-weights.json` with `expansionWeight` changed and every other key byte-identical:

- `exp_lo` = `simulator/placement/arms/sp3_expansion_lo.json` at 0.1
- `exp_hi` = `simulator/placement/arms/sp3_expansion_hi.json` at 0.3

Reference `base` = `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json`, the shipped vector. Both values sit inside the committed `sweep-bounds.json` range for this axis (`min` 0.0, `max` 1.2), and `simulator/crates/engine/src/policy/params_file.rs::the_sp3_arm_files_are_the_committed_expansion_perturbations` pins each file as the live defaults with only that key moved. A site's value is a marginal score on the candidate's own scale, so at 0.3 the two-site sum puts the term near a quarter of a typical candidate total and at 0.1 near a twelfth: 0.3 is the value large enough to be visible if the effect is real, 0.1 the conservative bracket below it. Two arms rather than one because the axis is new and its scale is a guess from the units, not from a prior measurement; a single point that read `equivalent` would leave open whether the value was simply too small. The two arms share the reference, which is what the paired estimator wants, and each is read against `base` on its own; no multiplicity correction is applied and none is claimed, because the disposition rule below takes the better-reading arm rather than testing a family hypothesis.

**Why this A/B is informative, and what a null would mean.** M-48 measured the outcome side of this quantity directly and found it large: boxing, the count of legal expansion sites a completed pair can still reach within two road builds, separates the outer quartiles by +6.46pp pooled and is positive at every slot, from **+5.54pp at slot 3** to +13.95pp at slot 2. That is the second largest placement-time reading in the programme and it is what powers this run. What M-48 does not establish is that a formula term can capture it. Two things could break the chain. The correlation may be downstream rather than causal: a pair with many reachable sites may simply be a pair on a good part of the board, in which case the existing production terms already price whatever wins the games and this term double-counts them. And the field may already reach open pairs by accident, since a high-production candidate on an empty board tends to sit where the fan is wide, leaving no headroom for an explicit term. A `better` reading says the formula was genuinely blind to expansion and paying for it wins games. A null at declared power says the boxing correlation is carried by quantities the formula already has, which is a real answer to `SIM-GAP-34` and the one M-36 would predict.

**Why this field.** A formula weight has to be scored against the live formula, so field and reference are both the app-formula spec over `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, per the plan's protocol and following M-51's second invocation, M-52 and M-53. Every seat runs the post-SP1 composite `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with player trading, the field whose provenance M-51 established; the hero seat differs from the other three in its placement weights alone. One consequence is specific to this term and is stated so the reading is not over-read later: the term is occupancy-dependent, so the hero's own picks are scored against a board the other three seats fill with the shipped formula's choices, and the run measures the term against that field rather than against a field where every seat expands deliberately.

**Design.** `evaluate`, `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 16,000 paired units over 2000 clusters, `--threads 0`, `--threshold 0.01`, `--alpha 0.05`, reference `base`. That is the plan's screen power for an optional term, matching M-38's placement screens, M-49, M-52 and M-53.

**Power.** The magnitude this run is powered against is **+5.54pp, M-48's smallest per-slot boxing gap (slot 3)**, not the +6.46pp pooled row. The pooled row is the wrong number for two reasons M-48 records: the snake completes pairs at pick indices 4 through 7, so the site count's scale moves with board fill and the pooled row mixes four fill levels, and the entry says in terms that SP3 should power its arm off a per-slot magnitude. Taking the smallest of the four is the conservative choice and it is also the honest one, because a single unscaled weight has to work at every slot at once; SP4's `slotScales` block exists precisely because it may not. Only a fraction of that 5.54pp is available to a formula term, since the term moves the pick rather than the outcome and moves it only where the expansion ranking disagrees with the production ranking. M-38's placement screens at this power resolved effects around 1pp with intervals of roughly `±0.4pp`, so an effect of a few tenths of a point is resolvable here and anything at or above 1pp is comfortably so. **Task 1's per-hero-seat table is where the slot pattern will be read**: it reports a `PairStats` per hero seat beside the pooled one, using the same estimator and both intervals, and it is record-only. It is the reading of record for whether an arm's effect is concentrated at the early slots, where the fan is widest and the pooled estimate dilutes it, and that pattern is what SP4's profile arms are designed off. The pooled verdict is still the verdict.

**Command.** Build first, from the repo root, so no compile lands inside a timed window:

```text
RUSTFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo build --release --manifest-path simulator/Cargo.toml -p unsettled-sim
```

Then, from `simulator/`, with `uptime` recorded immediately before and immediately after:

```text
target/release/unsettled-sim evaluate --layout standard4 --seats 4 --domain tuning --field app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --arm base=app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --arm exp_lo=app_formula:placement/arms/sp3_expansion_lo.json --arm exp_hi=app_formula:placement/arms/sp3_expansion_hi.json --reference base --boards 2000 --reps 2 --policy heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial --threads 0 --player-trading --threshold 0.01 --alpha 0.05 --out runs/m54-sp3-expansion
```

**Decision rule (fixed before the run).** The plan's standing disposition rule, applied per arm without discretion. `better` records that arm's value as a surviving SP6 candidate. `worse` drops it and the M entry records why the term was expected to help. `equivalent` at this declared power drops it and records the null itself as the answer to `SIM-GAP-34`'s arithmetic half. `inconclusive` buys units exactly once, at four times the boards (`--boards 8000`, `--reps` unchanged at 2) in a single retry invocation carrying both arms, and if the retry is still `inconclusive` the item is recorded unresolved and the run stops there. **The survivor is the better-reading arm if either reads `better`**: if both read `better`, the survivor is the one with the larger point estimate, and if that is a tie to the last recorded digit, the smaller weight, on the standing preference for the more conservative value. If neither reads `better` there is no survivor and the axis carries no value into SP6. The surviving value, or its absence, is recorded explicitly in the last paragraph of the M entry, because Task 9's decay sweep and Task 18's SP4 expansion profiles both read it and both are conditional on a `better` reading here. Record only in every branch: no shipped default moves, in `src/engine/weights.ts`, `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, `simulator/placement/phase-i-candidate-weights.json` or anywhere else, and the two arm files stay the only committed files carrying a nonzero value on this axis. Nothing here reverts the term either: a weight shipped at 0 skips the walk and is inert, and `SIM-GAP-34` stays open in `gaps.md` in every branch because that gap closes on adoption and adoption is SP6's business.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** I predict `exp_hi` at 0.3 reads `better` or `inconclusive`-positive with a point estimate around `+0.5pp`, and `exp_lo` at 0.1 reads `equivalent` with a smaller positive estimate around `+0.2pp`, on 700 to 1,400 discordant units of 16,000 for `exp_hi` and fewer for `exp_lo`. Four things drive that. First, the size of the underlying reading: +5.54pp at the worst slot is far larger than the effects the surviving formula weights carry, and even a small fraction of it converting is a positive point estimate. Second, the direction is not in doubt in the way M-53's was: nothing in the formula currently opposes expansion, so unlike the port deficit this term is not fighting a prior the field has already ruled on. Third, the reason to expect only a fraction: on an empty board the production argmax and the expansion argmax mostly agree, so the term changes the pick on a minority of candidates, which is also why I expect the discordant count to sit near M-53's 960 rather than far above it. Fourth, the reason `exp_lo` should read smaller: at 0.1 the term is roughly a twelfth of a candidate total, below the margin between the top few candidates on most boards, so it should rarely change the argmax at all. I would be mildly surprised by a negative point estimate on either arm, and a `worse` reading at 0.3 would be genuinely informative: it would mean the two-site sum is double-counting production the base terms already pay for, which is the failure mode the design note anticipates and the one M-36 would predict.

**Threshold and alpha.** `±1pp` practical threshold, alpha 0.05. Interval selection is the harness's own rule in `evaluate.rs::try_paired_stats`, the wider of the McNemar and board-clustered intervals.

**Admissibility.** The release binary is built before the measurement starts and the build is confirmed to report `Finished` against a warm fingerprint, which is M-50's lesson. `uptime` is recorded immediately before and immediately after the single `evaluate` invocation and both readings enter the M entry. Zero illegal actions required. The invocation shares its command block with no build, no test suite and no other CPU-heavy job. One run of 48,000 games, three arms against 2000 boards x 2 reps. Expect it to be materially slower per game than M-53's 7,226 games/s: two of the three arms carry a nonzero `expansionWeight`, and a nonzero weight enters the outward walk on every scored candidate, which is the first component in the formula that is not arithmetic on precomputed vertex data. Timing is a throughput observation only; the outcomes are deterministic in the seeds and unaffected by load. Should the extrapolated wall time exceed five minutes the plan's long-run rule applies, though a formula screen at this power is not expected to approach it. The reference arm's win rate is checked against the symmetric 0.25 corner, as M-51 through M-53 checked theirs. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so the artifact stays out of the tree and the M entry is the record.

**Numbering.** M-53 is the last entry in `.claude/specs/simulator/measurements.md` at this commit, so this run takes M-54. If that file has moved on by the time the run executes, the entry takes the next unused number and this preregistration is cited by filename.
