# M-52 preregistration — SP2a dev-card recipe bonus

Date: 2026-09-01. Committed before the run.

**Change under test.** SP2a added `recipeDevCardBonus` to the placement formula, closing the arithmetic half of `SIM-GAP-36`: `recipeRoadBonus`, `recipeCityBonus` and `recipeSettlementBonus` price the three building costs and the ore/wheat/sheep development-card cost had no counterpart. The term is `recipeDevCardBonus * min(oreRecipe, wheatRecipe, sheepRecipe)` inside `valuation.ts::diversityScore` and its mirror in `app_formula.rs::diversity_score`, built from the same `recipeCap` coverage the other three recipes use and landing in the existing `diversity` breakdown component. It ships at 0, so it prices nothing today and no committed vector's behaviour moved when it landed. This run asks the only question that can move it off 0: does pricing the dev-card recipe at placement time win more games?

**The arm.** One decision arm, `devcard` = `simulator/placement/arms/sp2a_devcard.json`, which is the live `simulator/placement/default-weights.json` with `recipeDevCardBonus` at 1.0 and every other key byte-identical. Reference `base` = `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json`, the shipped vector. 1.0 is `recipeSettlementBonus`'s value, and the settlement recipe is the other multi-resource recipe in the formula, so the candidate prices the dev-card recipe at the same maximum credit the formula already gives the four-resource one. It sits inside the committed `sweep-bounds.json` range for this axis (`min` 0.0, `max` 4.0). Single arm by design: one question, one contrast, no multiplicity to state.

**Why this A/B is informative, and what a null would mean.** This is the only term in the whole placement programme that raises sheep's standing on its own. Sheep currently earns credit through exactly two channels: the diversity spread, which pays for holding any of a resource, and the settlement recipe, whose minimum is over four resources and is therefore usually pinned by wood or brick rather than by sheep. The dev-card recipe's minimum is over three, and the ore/wheat pair that gates it is the same pair the city recipe already rewards, so the term's marginal signal is precisely "this pair also has sheep on top of an ore and wheat base". A `better` reading says the formula under-prices sheep at placement time. A null reads as the substantive answer that sheep's existing credit through the spread and the settlement recipe is already enough, and that reading is worth having on the record whichever way it goes, because the same question sits under any future proposal to re-rank `resourceValue.sheep`.

**Why this field.** A formula weight has to be scored against the live formula, so field and reference are both the app-formula spec over `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, per the plan's protocol and following M-51's second invocation. Every seat runs the post-SP1 composite `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with player trading, which is the field M-51 established the provenance of; the hero seat differs from the other three in its placement weights alone. Scoring the arm against a `pip_diversity` field instead would mix a formula-versus-heuristic gap into the contrast, which is what M-38's reference win rate of `0.2331875` against the symmetric 0.25 corner shows happening.

**Design.** `evaluate`, `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 16,000 paired units over 2000 clusters, `--threads 0`, `--threshold 0.01`, `--alpha 0.05`, reference `base`. That is the plan's screen power for an optional term, matching M-38's placement screens and M-49.

**Command.** Build first, from the repo root, so no compile lands inside a timed window:

```text
RUSTFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo build --release --manifest-path simulator/Cargo.toml -p unsettled-sim
```

Then, from `simulator/`, with `uptime` recorded immediately before and immediately after:

```text
target/release/unsettled-sim evaluate --layout standard4 --seats 4 --domain tuning --field app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --arm base=app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --arm devcard=app_formula:placement/arms/sp2a_devcard.json --reference base --boards 2000 --reps 2 --policy heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial --threads 0 --player-trading --threshold 0.01 --alpha 0.05 --out runs/m52-sp2a
```

**Decision rule (fixed before the run).** The plan's standing disposition rule, applied without discretion. `better` records 1.0 as a surviving SP6 candidate. `worse` drops the candidate and the M entry records why the term was expected to help. `equivalent` at this declared power drops the candidate and records the null itself as the answer to the sheep question above. `inconclusive` buys units exactly once, at four times the boards (`--boards 8000`, `--reps` unchanged at 2), and if the retry is still `inconclusive` the item is recorded unresolved and the run stops there. Record only in every branch: no shipped default moves, in `src/engine/weights.ts`, `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, `simulator/placement/phase-i-candidate-weights.json` or anywhere else, and `sp2a_devcard.json` is the only committed file carrying a nonzero value on this axis. Nothing here reverts the term either: a weight shipped at 0 is arithmetically inert, removing it is not in SP2's scope, and `SIM-GAP-36` stays open in `gaps.md` in every branch because the gap closes on adoption and adoption is SP6's business.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** I predict `equivalent`, with a point estimate inside +-0.3pp and a discordant count in the low hundreds out of 16,000. The reasoning is collinearity plus the closest available analogue. Collinearity: the term's three resources are ore and wheat, which the city recipe already rewards jointly, and sheep, which the spread already pays for, so the term re-scores mostly along directions the formula prices already and should reorder few candidate vertices. Analogue: M-38's `settlement_hi` raised `recipeSettlementBonus` from 1 to 2, adding the same 1.0 of maximum headroom this arm adds, and read `+0.12pp` on 111 versus 92 discordant units, with `settlement_lo` at `-0.08pp` on 33 versus 46. At a few hundred discordant units the selected interval should be roughly +-0.2 to +-0.3pp, comfortably inside the practical threshold, which is what makes `equivalent` rather than `inconclusive` the expected verdict. Direction, held loosely: slightly positive, because ore-wheat-sheep is a real opening and nothing in the formula names it. I would be surprised by anything outside +-1pp, and a `better` reading at this power would be the largest single-term formula effect the programme has measured.

**Threshold and alpha.** +-1pp practical threshold, alpha 0.05. Interval selection is the harness's own rule in `evaluate.rs::try_paired_stats`, the wider of the McNemar and board-clustered intervals.

**Admissibility.** The release binary is built before the measurement starts and the build is confirmed to report `Finished` against a warm fingerprint, which is M-50's lesson. `uptime` is recorded immediately before and immediately after the single `evaluate` invocation and both readings enter the M entry. Zero illegal actions required. The invocation shares its command block with no build, no test suite and no other CPU-heavy job. One run of 32,000 games, expected around 5s at the observed throughput of about 7,000 games/s and far inside the 5-minute ceiling. The reference arm's win rate is checked against the symmetric 0.25 corner, as M-51 checked its two. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so the artifact stays out of the tree and the M entry is the record.

**Numbering.** M-51 is the last entry in `.claude/specs/simulator/measurements.md` at this commit, so this run takes M-52. If that file has moved on by the time the run executes, the entry takes the next unused number and this preregistration is cited by filename.
