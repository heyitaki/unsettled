# M-49 preregistration: `SIM-GAP-33` contest block-bonus predicate

Date: 2026-09-01. Committed before the run.

**Change under test.** `denial.rs::contest_term` scales a rival's danger contribution by `1 + contest_block_bonus` when the candidate edge is that rival's last remaining one-road approach to the contested vertex. Until commit `65203baa` the predicate decided "blocked" from build legality alone, so a rival that already owned an edge incident to the contested vertex passed it, because an owned edge is not legal to build, while still being able to settle at that vertex with no new road at all. The predicate now additionally requires that the rival owns no incident edge. `contest_cap`, `contest_block_bonus` and the rest of the positional-competition arithmetic are untouched. This measures what that correction cost or bought in played games.

**The arm is the code change, not a parameter.** The predicate is not expressible as a single-parameter perturbation. `contest_block_bonus` is a scalar multiplier on the bonus, so setting it to 0 removes blocking pricing entirely rather than restoring the loose predicate, and no other key in `DenialParams` distinguishes the two predicates. The repository's one mechanism for holding pre-change behaviour inside a single binary is `heuristic_v1.rs::LegacyValuation`, reachable only through a named `PolicyKind` such as `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial-legacyrace`; a params file cannot carry it, because `params_file.rs::parse_params_file` pins `legacyValuation` to null by design, legacy restorations being measurement labels rather than swept parameters. The `SIM-GAP-33` fix landed without minting a legacy flag, so no single build contains both predicates. The reference is therefore the pre-change binary and this run uses a two-build protocol, stated in full below.

**The two builds.** Pre-change: commit `47b1f39a`, checked out into a detached scratch worktree. Post-change: the tree at this preregistration's commit. The only compiled delta between them is `simulator/crates/engine/src/policy/denial.rs` and its test file; every other file changed between the two commits is markdown. Both are built `--release` on the same toolchain, and `meta.json` in each run records the `rustc` version so a toolchain mismatch is visible in the artifacts.

**Why the programme's usual field cannot be used here.** The measurement protocol puts every seat on `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` and differentiates the hero seat by its placement arm. That works for a placement weight and, through `--arm-policy`, for a policy parameter, because in both cases the hero can differ from the field inside one binary. A code change cannot: within a build every seat carries it, so an all-composite field moves all four seats identically and the hero's win rate stays at the symmetric baseline whatever the predicate does. The reading would be a guaranteed null that says nothing. The design therefore follows M-26 (`docs/plans/preregs/2026-08-25-m26-race-cap-blocking.md`), the previous A/B on this same contest term: the composite sits on the hero seat alone and the field runs a policy without the denial component. The field here is `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards`, the shipped composite minus exactly the component under test, which is the strongest denial-free field available and closer to the programme's field than M-26's `heuristic-v1-trader`.

**Design.** Two runs, one per build, identical in every argument. `evaluate`, `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, placement field `pip_diversity`, `--player-trading`, `--threads 0`, `--threshold 0.01`, `--alpha 0.05`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 16,000 paired units over 2000 clusters per run. Two arms per run, both on placement `pip_diversity` so the placement is not a variable: `base` puts the hero on the field policy `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards`, and `block` puts the hero on `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial`, the code under test. `base` names its policy explicitly through `--arm-policy` even though it equals `--policy`, so the artifact's `armPolicies` map records both arms. Reference is `base` in both runs.

**Why the `base` arm is the anchor.** `denial::contest_term` is reached only through the `gated` option in `heuristic_v1.rs`, which is `None` for any policy without the denial component, so no seat in the `base` arm executes the changed code. The `base` arm's 16,000 games are consequently identical in the two builds, down to their outcomes. That gives the two runs one shared reference vector, which is what makes their estimates differenceable, and it is also the run's sharpest admissibility check: `games`, `wins` and `draws` for `base` must match exactly across the two `evaluation.json` files, and any mismatch means something other than the predicate moved and voids the run.

**Estimand.** Each run reports `est = p(block) - p(base)`, the denial component's edge over the denial-free composite under that build's predicate. The quantity under test is the difference of the two, `delta = est_post - est_pre`, which because `base` is identical across builds equals `p(block_post) - p(block_pre)`: the predicate's effect on the hero's win rate, holding the field, the boards and the seeds fixed.

**How `delta`'s interval is formed.** The two runs are separate processes and `evaluation.json` carries no per-unit indicator vector, so the exact discordance between `block_pre` and `block_post` is not recoverable and McNemar cannot be applied to `delta` directly. `delta`'s interval is therefore the two selected half-widths combined in quadrature, `h = sqrt(h_pre^2 + h_post^2)`, centred on `delta`. That is conservative rather than exact: the two estimates are strongly positively correlated, sharing an identical `base` vector and differing only on the units where the predicate actually fires, so the true paired variance of their difference is smaller than the independent sum this uses. The verdict on `delta` applies the same rule the harness applies in `evaluate.rs::try_paired_stats`: `better` if the interval lies strictly above +1pp, `worse` if strictly below -1pp, `equivalent` if it lies strictly inside +-1pp, `inconclusive` otherwise. Each run's own `block` verdict is recorded alongside, but it answers a different question (does the denial component beat a denial-free field) and is not the decision.

**Power, and its honest limit.** Screen power is the plan's 2000 boards x 2 reps per build, 32,000 games per run. The limitation to state before the numbers exist: each run's interval carries the variance of the whole denial component, not of the predicate alone, and quadrature then widens it further, so `delta`'s interval may well be too wide to sit inside +-1pp even though the predicate itself fires rarely. `inconclusive` is a realistic outcome of this design and is not a reason to buy units. The plan's four-times-boards clause exists for an optional term whose disposition depends on the reading; this fix lands whatever the reading is, so more units would buy nothing that changes an action.

**Decision rule (fixed before the run).** This is a correctness fix and it lands regardless of the verdict. `better` or `equivalent` on `delta` records the fix as positive or costless. `worse` records the loss, quantifies it, and flags it for the user without reverting the change, per the plan's gap-fix rule. `inconclusive` records the effect as unresolved at screen power and says so plainly in the M entry, next to the corpus evidence on how rarely the predicate fires. Nothing is adopted in any branch: no shipped default changes in `src/engine/weights.ts`, `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, `simulator/placement/policy-default-params.json`, or any file under `simulator/placement/arms/`.

**Transferability, stated as a limitation.** The reading is the predicate's effect on one seat playing the composite against three seats that do not. It is not the effect in the shipped configuration where every seat is denial-gated and the correction applies to all of them at once. No design available in this binary can measure that, since a symmetric field cancels the change by construction. The M entry records the reading with this scope attached, and no later phase may cite it as a measurement of the all-composite field.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** The corpus recapture behind the fix moved 7 of 600 games on `standard4` and 25 of 600 on `extension6`, only in the denial-gated composite and in no ungated policy, so the predicate changes a decision rarely. M-26 measured the entire blocking bonus at `+0.00025` with only 186 of 16,000 units discordant, and this change withdraws a subset of that bonus's credit rather than adding a new term, so its effect should be smaller still. I predict `delta` inside +-0.3pp with a mildly positive point estimate, on the reasoning that the loose predicate paid a blocking premium for roads that block nobody and the fix stops spending on them. I expect the verdict to read `equivalent` if the combined interval is tight enough to contain and `inconclusive` otherwise, and I would be surprised by any point estimate outside +-1pp. A `worse` reading would say the over-credit was accidentally load-bearing, which is worth knowing and is the case that gets flagged.

**Threshold and alpha.** +-1pp practical threshold, alpha 0.05. Interval selection inside each run is the harness's own rule, the wider of the McNemar and board-clustered intervals.

**Admissibility.** Both binaries are built before either measurement starts, so no compile overlaps a timed run. `uptime` is recorded immediately before and immediately after each of the two `evaluate` invocations, and all four readings enter the M entry. Zero illegal actions required in both runs. The two invocations run one at a time, never in the same command block as each other, a build, or a test suite. The `base` arm identity check across the two artifacts must pass. Each run is 32,000 games, expected well under the 5-minute ceiling at the observed throughput. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so both artifacts stay out of the tree and the M entry is the record. The scratch worktree is removed after the run.

