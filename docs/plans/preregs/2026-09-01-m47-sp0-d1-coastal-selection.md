# M-47 preregistration: SP0-D1 coastal selection

Date: 2026-09-01. Committed before the run.

**What this measures.** Whether the shipped placement formula systematically prefers fewer, fatter hexes over more, thinner ones once pip total is held fixed, and whether that preference costs games. `SIM-GAP-35` is the surface: the setup grant pays one card per adjacent producing hex regardless of token, so hex count is a quantity the board pays on independently of pip total, and `handValueWeight` is 0 in the adopted defaults (`simulator/placement/default-weights.json`), which leaves hex count entering the formula only through the diversity, coverage, duplicate-number and port terms, none of which is about the grant. D1 sizes that gap before SP2c decides whether to build a term for it. Over every setup pick a game makes, the diagnostic rebuilds the ownership state as it stood immediately before the pick, re-scores every legal candidate vertex through `placement::setup_candidate_score`, which is the entry point the pick itself went through and which forwards an app-formula placement to `AppFormulaScorer::score_for_owner`, with the same `grant` flag the pick carried, and takes the chosen vertex against the best-scoring unchosen legal alternative whose pip total is within one of the chosen vertex's. Hex count throughout means the number of adjacent hexes carrying both a resource and a token; pip total means the sum of pips over those hexes. A pick with no such alternative is skipped, and a pair whose two hex counts are equal is discarded, since a tie expresses no preference and only dilutes the share.

**Why the pip-matched runner-up construction makes 50% the null.** The pair is built so that its two members are matched on the only quantity the formula's base term reads: pip total, to within one pip. A formula blind to hex count therefore orders that pair on grounds unrelated to hex count, which leaves the chosen vertex and the runner-up exchangeable with respect to which of them carries the lower hex count. Under that exchangeability the share of pairs in which the lower hex count won is a fair coin, so 50% is the null the construction forces rather than a number chosen for convenience, and any reliable departure from even odds is a real preference. "Reliable" is decided by the interval, clustered by board at the run's declared `alpha`, as the paired-statistics contract in `.claude/specs/simulator/contracts.md` requires: picks from one board share that board's geometry and are not independent observations.

**Design.** One `diagnose` invocation, `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 4000 games and 32,000 setup picks before the skip and tie filters. Every seat plays the same placement and the same policy: `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json` and `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with `--player-trading`, so the reading is of the shipped formula under the adopted field rather than of a contrast between two arms. `--alpha 0.05`. Reported overall and per draft slot, meaning the picking seat's index, so a slot effect cannot hide inside an average. The same invocation also produces both SP0-D2 readings, preregistered in `docs/plans/preregs/2026-09-01-m48-sp0-d2-expansion-blockability.md`; the three readings share one run because they are three statistics over one set of observed games, not three experiments.

**Command** (from `simulator/`):

```text
cargo run --release -p unsettled-sim -- diagnose --layout standard4 --seats 4 --domain tuning --placement app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --boards 2000 --reps 2 --policy heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial --player-trading --alpha 0.05 --threads 0 --out runs/m47-m48-sp0-diagnostic
```

**This is a diagnostic, not an A/B.** It has no arms, no reference, no field and no hero-seat rotation, so it returns no verdict and the programme's paired-statistics disposition rule (`better` keeps, `worse` drops, `equivalent` at power drops, `inconclusive` buys units once) does not apply to it and cannot be read onto it. What it produces is one boolean per phase decision: D1's gate boolean for SP2c, and D2's branch boolean for the dropped robber-attraction term. Nothing here adopts or changes a shipped default in either direction.

**Passing condition for SP2c (fixed before the run).** SP2c runs if and only if both of the following hold on the overall row, and the run emits exactly that conjunction as `coastalSelection.sp2cGatePassed` in `diagnostics.json`:

- The share of pairs in which the lower hex count was chosen sits above 50% by more than its interval, meaning the clustered interval's lower bound is strictly above 0.5 and the interval is not degenerate.
- The picks that took the lower hex count won at least 1 percentage point less often than the picks that took the higher one, meaning `winRateGap`, defined as the higher-hex win rate minus the lower-hex win rate, is at least 0.01.

Both conditions, not either. A preference the win rate does not punish is a preference, not a bias worth a term, and a win-rate difference without a preference is not evidence about the formula. The 1pp is a registered threshold this programme is entitled to set; what it must not be is discovered after the run, which is what this file prevents. If the gate fails, Tasks 24 through 26 of this run are skipped on the recorded verdict and SP2c is closed as not indicated, not as unresolved. The per-slot rows are read but do not enter the gate: the gate is the overall row, declared here so a favourable slot cannot be selected after the fact.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** I expect the gate to fail on its first condition, with the share at or somewhat below 50%. The reasoning: with `handValueWeight` at 0 nothing in the formula rewards hex count as such, while two terms that do fire reward it indirectly. Diversity and coverage both pay for distinct resources, and among pip-matched candidates the cheapest way to hold more distinct resources is to touch more hexes, so both should push the formula toward the higher hex count. Pushing the other way, the port terms fire disproportionately on coastal vertices, which are exactly the low-hex-count ones, but only on the subset carrying or neighbouring a port, so the pull is narrower than the push. I therefore expect the formula's residual preference to lean slightly toward the higher hex count rather than the lower one, which is the opposite of the bias SP2c would correct. On the second condition I expect a small `winRateGap`, under 1pp in magnitude and of uncertain sign, because pip matching removes the dominant driver of outcome from the contrast. If the prediction is wrong and the share sits reliably above 50%, that is the interesting case: it would mean the formula prefers concentration on grounds the win rate does not support, which is precisely what a hex-count tempo term would price.

**Threshold and alpha.** Alpha 0.05, intervals clustered by board over 2000 clusters, computed through the same `simulator/crates/cli/src/stats.rs` machinery the A/B runs use. The 1pp win-rate threshold above. No practical-equivalence threshold applies, because there is no arm and no equivalence claim to make.

**Admissibility.** `uptime` recorded immediately before and immediately after the command, both entering the M entry. Zero illegal actions required. No concurrent test suite, cargo build, or other CPU-heavy job in the same block or in parallel with it. One invocation of 4000 games plus per-pick candidate re-scoring, expected to complete in well under the 5-minute ceiling. `diagnostics.json` is deterministic and carries no elapsed time or worker count, so a repeat at any `--threads` is byte-identical and the artifact itself is not evidence of load; `uptime` is. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so the artifact stays out of the tree and the M entry is the record.
