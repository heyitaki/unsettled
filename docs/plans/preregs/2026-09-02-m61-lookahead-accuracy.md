# M-61 preregistration: greedy lookahead accuracy against the draft-aware field

Date: 2026-09-02. Committed before the run.

**What this measures.** How well the draft-aware kind's opponent model predicts the field it is about to become. `simulator/crates/engine/src/placement/draft.rs` prices a first settlement by replaying the picks that follow it, and it replays them greedily: every intervening seat takes the argmax of the opponent formula. Against a greedy field that replay is exact and `simulator/crates/cli/tests/draft_lookahead.rs::the_lookahead_predicts_the_picks_the_field_actually_made` pins it. Against a field where every seat is itself draft-aware it is an approximation, and this run is the number for how good an approximation, taken on games the field actually played rather than argued from the code.

**Why it is needed now.** The SP6 decisions the user made on 2026-09-02, recorded as `## SP6 decisions, 2026-09-02` in `.claude/specs/simulator/placement-programme.md`, make the draft-aware kind the programme's reference field and keep the hero on the greedy opponent model, with a recursive model out of scope. The same note says the accuracy of that approximation is measured and recorded rather than assumed, which is this entry. The field switch rests on M-59 and on this reading and lands whatever the SP6 weight package does, so nothing here gates anything: what this run changes is how honestly every later SP6 entry can be worded about the field it was taken on.

**This is a diagnostic, not an A/B.** It has no field, no arms, no reference and no hero-seat rotation, so it returns no verdict, and the programme's disposition rule (`better` keeps, `worse` drops, `equivalent` at power drops, `inconclusive` buys units once) does not apply to it and cannot be read onto it. `diagnose` takes no `--threshold` flag, and SP6's 0.5pp practical bar recorded in `.claude/specs/simulator/contracts.md` governs the paired verdicts of the A/B runs and not this one. Nothing here adopts anything or moves a shipped default in either direction: `src/engine/weights.ts`, `simulator/placement/default-weights.json` and every arm file stay exactly as they are.

**The statistic.** `simulator/crates/cli/src/lookahead.rs`, added by Task 4 of `docs/plans/completed/simulator-placement-sp6.md` and reported in the `lookahead` block of `diagnostics.json`. For every recorded first settlement, the lookahead runs from the board as it stood immediately before that pick, with the vertex the seat actually took, and the plan it forms is held against the trace. It reports three shares, overall and per draft slot: `pickShare`, the share of intervening picks the model named exactly, seat and vertex alike, in position; `sequenceShare`, the share of first picks whose whole intervening sequence it named, counted only over the first picks that have an intervening pick at all; and `secondShare`, the share of second settlements that landed on the site the lookahead planned. Every quantity is a count, so the block is byte-identical across worker counts. Nothing is chosen by the statistic, the games are already played, so it runs on whatever `--placement` names and the two invocations below are two calls into one code path.

**The structure the shares are read against, which is fixed by `game.rs::setup_order` and not by the placement.** At 4 seats the order is 0, 1, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1, 0, so the picks between a seat's two settlements number 6 at slot 0, 4 at slot 1, 2 at slot 2 and none at slot 3, and exactly half of them at every slot are second-round picks. Slot 3 has nothing to predict, so its `pickShare` and `sequenceShare` read 0 by the statistic's zero-denominator convention rather than because the model failed, and `secondShare` is the only one of the three that means anything there. Per slot, `sequenceShare` cannot exceed `pickShare`, because a slot's intervening count is a constant and every exact sequence contributes all of its picks to the matched count; a violation is an arithmetic bug rather than a reading.

**Two invocations, and the mismatch means a different thing in each.** The control puts every seat on `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json`. There the replay is the field, exactly: the model's greedy argmax is what each seat runs, at the same weights, with the grant applied on the second round by `draft.rs::replay` exactly as the field applies it. The only thing that can separate them is the tie-break, since `placement/mod.rs::choose_app_formula` breaks a settlement tie with a draw from the picking seat's own policy stream while `draft.rs::best_legal` takes the lowest vertex index. **So the control's shortfall from 1 is pure tie noise and carries no information about the opponent model.** The reading puts every seat on `app_formula_draft:placement/default-weights.json@placement/default-weights.json`. There the field draws nothing from the policy stream during setup, by `draft.rs`'s own determinism, so there is no tie noise at all; and the rivals' second-round picks are `draft.rs::best_direct` at the same weights with the grant on, which is what the replay predicts them with, so those are exact given that the board the replay has reached still matches the trace. **The whole of the reading's shortfall is therefore the first-round picks, where the field runs a lookahead and the model stands a greedy argmax in for it, plus the propagation of a first-round miss through every later position.**

**The control is a bug check and a tie-floor sizing, and it is not a baseline to subtract.** The two shortfalls have different sources, tie noise in one and model error in the other, and the reading's own tie noise is zero rather than the control's figure. The entry may not net one against the other, and this is stated before the numbers exist so it cannot be done after.

**The exactness condition, and how it is enforced.** On a greedy field with no tie anywhere the model is the field itself, so every share must read exactly 1; anything else says the statistic is broken rather than that the model is. That condition is pinned by the committed `simulator/crates/cli/tests/draft_lookahead.rs::a_tie_free_greedy_field_is_predicted_exactly`, which screens tie-free traced games by re-scoring every legal candidate at every pick and requires all three shares to read exactly 1 over them. This run is not tie-screened, so the control reads below 1 and how far below is the tie floor rather than a test. **The in-run exactness check is on the reading, not the control: the draft-aware run's slot 3 `secondShare` must read exactly 1.0.** At slot 3 the hero's second settlement follows its first with nothing in between, so the state the plan projects and the state the field picks from are the same state, and both sides take the same hero formula with the grant on and the same lowest-index rule. A slot 3 `secondShare` below 1 means the statistic is not reading the state it claims to, and **makes the run inadmissible**.

**Registered structural counts, identical in both runs.** Both invocations play the same board set, and the intervening counts are set by the seat order rather than by the placement, so both must report `firstPicks` 16,000 (4 seats over 4,000 games), `predictingFirstPicks` 12,000 (slots 0, 1 and 2) and `interveningPicks` 48,000 (12 per game). A mismatch between the two runs on any of the three, or against these figures, means the trace or the schedule moved and **makes the run inadmissible**.

**Design.** `diagnose`, `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning2`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 4,000 games and 16,000 first settlements per invocation, every seat on `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with `--player-trading`, `--alpha 0.05`, `--threads 0`. `tuning2` is SP6's screening domain, minted by Task 2 of `docs/plans/completed/simulator-placement-sp6.md` with seed `8796197394747623682`; `tuning`, `eval` and `gate` are spent and no invocation here carries them, and `eval2` and `gate2` are untouched by this run. The power is M-47's and M-48's, which is the plan's diagnostic power, and it is a share of 48,000 predicted picks rather than a paired contrast, so no interval and no threshold enter the readings; `--alpha` is carried because the coastal and expansion blocks in the same artifact use it and they are not this entry's subject.

**Timing, preregistered before the run.** M-47's `diagnose` at this exact power took 0.78s at 18 workers for 4,000 games, which is 5,128 games/s with the coastal replay included, so the control adds only the statistic's own work: one single-candidate replay per first settlement, four per game, each a small fraction of the full lookahead that scores every candidate. **The control is predicted under 2 seconds.** The reading puts a lookahead on all four seats. M-60 ran every game with one draft seat at 6,252.2 games/s against M-58's 7,465.3 for a greedy game, which puts one seat's lookahead near 26 microseconds of aggregate slot time; four of them plus M-47's diagnostic overhead puts the reading near 300 microseconds a game, or about 1.3 seconds for 4,000 games. **The reading is predicted under 5 seconds**, and both are far under the plan's eight-minute background-and-poll mark, so both run in the foreground. If either invocation exceeds 60 seconds the extrapolation is wrong by more than an order of magnitude and that fact is recorded in the entry; there is no branch to take, because 60 seconds is still a foreground run.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** The arithmetic first, so the numbers are a model rather than a guess. Write `m` for the per-pick rate at which the control's random tie-break disagrees with the lowest-index rule, and `a` for the per-state rate at which a greedy argmax names the same first settlement the draft kind's lookahead names. A pick at replay depth `j` matches only if it matches itself and the board reached it aligned, so its match rate is about `(1 - m)^j` on the control, and on the reading it is about `a^d` where `d` counts the first-round picks at or before that position. Summing over the 12 predicted positions a game holds, at `m` = 0.05 the control's `pickShare` works out near 0.87 and at `m` = 0.10 near 0.75; at `a` = 0.9 the reading's `pickShare` works out near 0.81, at `a` = 0.8 near 0.65 and at `a` = 0.7 near 0.51.

I predict the **control reads `pickShare` between 0.75 and 0.92** overall, with `sequenceShare` below it and `secondShare` between the two, and its **slot 3 `secondShare` between 0.90 and 0.99**, which is the cleanest estimate of `m` in the artifact because it is one pick with no propagation behind it. I predict the **reading comes in between 0.45 and 0.70 on `pickShare`**, on `sequenceShare` between 0.20 and 0.45, and on `secondShare` between 0.55 and 0.80, the last pulled up by slot 3 reading exactly 1.0. Per slot on the reading I predict a monotone ordering, slot 2 highest and slot 0 lowest, because the shortfall compounds with the number of first-round picks the replay has to stand a greedy argmax in for: 1 at slot 2, 2 at slot 1 and 3 at slot 0. The direction argument for `a` sitting near 0.8 rather than near 1 is M-59 itself, which found the draft kind worth `+1.6484pp` while diverging from greedy on well under half its units: a kind that changes few picks and wins a large share of the ones it changes is a kind whose argmax usually agrees with greedy, and `a` is exactly that agreement rate seen from the other side.

Two readings would change how the field switch is described and are named now so they cannot be explained away later. **A reading `pickShare` at or above the control's** would say the greedy approximation costs essentially nothing against a draft-aware field, so M-59's "correctly specified by construction" caveat all but transfers to the new field and later SP6 entries can say so. **A reading `pickShare` below 0.35** would say the model is badly wrong about the field it plays, so every SP6 arm is being scored by a hero whose lookahead is planning against a draft that does not happen; that does not block anything, since the field switch is a decision already made and this run has no verdict, but it would be recorded as a limitation on every later SP6 entry and flagged for the user.
