# M-48 preregistration — SP0-D2 expansion boxing and blockability

Date: 2026-09-01. Committed before the run.

**What this measures.** Two quantities read off every completed starting pair, each joined to how that seat's game ended, each gating a different later decision.

- **Boxing:** the number of legal expansion sites the completed pair can still reach within two road builds. A site is a vertex that is unoccupied and not adjacent to an occupied vertex at that moment; reachable means reachable from the seat's own road network by adding at most two roads, counting distances 0, 1 and 2. The walk follows `policy::frontier::opened` for what counts as an expansion target, but starts from the seat's road network rather than from a single vertex, because the quantity SP3 needs is the room the pair has rather than the room one settlement has. This sizes `SIM-GAP-34` and fixes the units SP3's A/B needs to resolve `equivalent` rather than `inconclusive` at whatever effect size it finds. The incidence of zero reachable sites is reported alongside the gap, since a threshold-shaped response would live in exactly that case, and a threshold response is what would force SP3 to split back into a gradient term plus a separate boxing penalty.
- **Blockability:** the share of the pair's total pips sitting on its single highest-pip hex, over the distinct producing hexes adjacent to either settlement, reported as 0 for a pair with no producing hexes. This is the quantity the robber-attraction term the programme dropped would have priced: an economy piled on one hex that one robber can take away. `duplicateNumberPenalty` does not price it, which is why the reading is needed. That term scores overlap between token numbers, so two separate hexes sharing a number are penalised exactly like one shared hex though only the shared hex falls to a single robber, and production piled on one hex with no repeated number is not penalised at all.

**Why both are read at pair completion rather than at game end.** Both quantities are properties of the starting position, which is the surface `SIM-GAP-34` and the dropped robber-attraction term are both about. The moment a seat's second setup pick lands is the last moment at which either quantity is still a fact about the placement alone. Read at game end, boxing would be measuring sixty turns of opponent roads and settlements crowding the seat out, and blockability would be measuring which hexes the seat went on to city up and where the robber actually sat, so both would price downstream play rather than the placement that a placement term has to be scored on. A term the app computes at draft time can only be justified by a quantity knowable at draft time.

**Why both are reported the same way.** Each is reported as the win rate of the top quartile of that quantity minus the win rate of the bottom quartile, with the arms cut at the quarter values rather than at ranks and both quantities cut identically. The construction has to be shared because the branch below compares the two magnitudes directly, and two gaps built differently would not be comparable. Value cuts rather than rank cuts because a rank cut would have to split the run of equal values straddling the boundary, and the only order available to split it by is observation order, which runs seat by seat within each game and would let a draft-slot effect leak into the contrast. Both quantities are reported overall and per draft slot, so a slot effect cannot hide inside an average.

**Design.** The same single `diagnose` invocation as M-47, preregistered in `docs/plans/preregs/2026-09-01-m47-sp0-d1-coastal-selection.md`: `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 4000 games and 16,000 completed pairs, every seat on `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with `--player-trading`, placement `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json`, `--alpha 0.05`, `--threads 0`. One run, three readings, because they are three statistics over one set of observed games rather than three experiments.

**Command** (from `simulator/`):

```text
cargo run --release -p unsettled-sim -- diagnose --layout standard4 --seats 4 --domain tuning --placement app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --boards 2000 --reps 2 --policy heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial --player-trading --alpha 0.05 --threads 0 --out runs/m47-m48-sp0-diagnostic
```

**This is a diagnostic, not an A/B.** It has no arms, no reference, no field and no hero-seat rotation, so it returns no verdict and the programme's paired-statistics disposition rule (`better` keeps, `worse` drops, `equivalent` at power drops, `inconclusive` buys units once) does not apply to it and cannot be read onto it. What it produces is one boolean per phase decision: D2's branch boolean for the robber-attraction term, and D1's gate boolean for SP2c. Nothing here adopts or changes a shipped default in either direction.

**Branch condition (fixed before the run).** The robber-attraction term the programme dropped is revisited if and only if the absolute blockability quartile gap exceeds the absolute boxing quartile gap on the overall row. The run emits exactly that comparison as `expansion.robberAttractionRevisit` in `diagnostics.json`. The condition is a comparison rather than a threshold because the term was dropped for being speculative next to five terms that are not, so what would change that is evidence that concentration separates outcomes at least as sharply as expansion room does, and expansion room is the item the programme already committed to building. Absolute values on both sides: a large negative blockability gap, concentration losing games, is exactly as much reason to price concentration as a large positive one, and the direction is what the term's sign would be read from rather than whether it exists. If the branch is true, SP3's scope note records the penalty as a separate item to be specified, and this run does not build it: SP3 is out of scope here.

**Second reading, recorded but not branching.** The zero-site incidence decides nothing by itself in this run. It is recorded because SP3's merge of the boxing penalty into one gradient term assumes the response to expansion room is a gradient, and if a large share of pairs sit at zero sites while the quartile gap is small, that pattern is a threshold response one linear term cannot carry. Whoever specifies SP3 reads it; no condition here turns on it, and that limitation is stated now rather than after the number is in hand.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** Boxing: I expect a positive gap of a few percentage points, top quartile over bottom, since more reachable expansion room means more settlements and more roads available at a lower cost, and I expect the zero-site incidence to be low, under 5%, because at pair completion only eight settlements and eight roads are on the board and a pair with two roads of its own has room in almost every configuration. Blockability: I expect a small gap of uncertain sign and smaller magnitude than boxing's. Concentration cuts both ways: a pair whose pips pile onto one hex is robber-exposed, which loses games, but the pips pile there because that hex is a 6 or an 8, which wins them, and the two effects partly cancel within a quantity that does not distinguish them. I therefore predict `robberAttractionRevisit` comes back false and the term stays dropped. The case that would change my mind is a blockability gap that is both large and negative, which would say concentration is costing games beyond what the strength of the fat hex returns.

**Threshold and alpha.** Alpha 0.05 is carried on the run for D1's clustered interval. The D2 quartile gaps are reported as point contrasts with their arm sizes, means and cut values, and the branch is a comparison of two magnitudes rather than a test against a null, so no threshold and no interval enter it. That is a deliberate limitation of a first sizing reading and is stated here so the M entry cannot later be cited as though the gaps carried significance: what they carry is size, which is what SP3's A/B needs to be powered.

**Admissibility.** `uptime` recorded immediately before and immediately after the command, both entering the M entry. Zero illegal actions required. No concurrent test suite, cargo build, or other CPU-heavy job in the same block or in parallel with it. One invocation of 4000 games, expected to complete in well under the 5-minute ceiling. `diagnostics.json` is deterministic and carries no elapsed time or worker count, so a repeat at any `--threads` is byte-identical and the artifact itself is not evidence of load; `uptime` is. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so the artifact stays out of the tree and the M entry is the record.
