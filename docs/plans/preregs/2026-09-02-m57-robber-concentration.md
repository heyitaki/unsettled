# M-57 preregistration: the robber concentration term

Date: 2026-09-02. Committed before the run.

**Change under test.** `robberConcentrationWeight`, added by Task 14 of `docs/plans/simulator-placement-sp3-sp5.md` on the branch M-56 opened. The formula already prices the hex the robber sits on today, through `robberDiscount`, and prices nothing about a pair's standing exposure to being blocked: two pairs with identical pips score identically whether their income comes evenly off six hexes or three quarters of it off one. The term is a delta on the existing `robber` component, `robberConcentrationWeight * (topHexShare(holdings + c) - topHexShare(holdings))` subtracted from the candidate's score, where `topHexShare` is the largest single hex's share of all the pips the *distinct* producing hexes touched by those settlements make, so a hex two settlements share counts once. It is computed by `src/engine/valuation.ts::concentrationDelta` over `topHexShare` and mirrored by `simulator/crates/engine/src/placement/app_formula.rs::concentration_delta` over its own `top_hex_share`, the two pinned against each other by parity class `W14` through cases `robber-concentration` and `robber-concentration-shared-hex`. The share is over raw pips rather than robber-adjusted ones, matching `simulator/crates/cli/src/expansion.rs::pair_hexes`, the diagnostic the quantity comes from, so the term prices a property of the board rather than of where the robber happens to be. It ships at 0, where neither scorer takes a share and the delta is exactly 0, which the byte-identical corpus diff in Task 14 confirmed. This run asks the only question that can move the weight off 0: does charging a candidate for concentrating a pair's income on one blockable hex win more games?

**The arm.** One decision arm, the live `simulator/placement/default-weights.json` with `robberConcentrationWeight` changed and every other key byte-identical:

- `conc` = `simulator/placement/arms/sp3_concentration.json` at 4.0

Reference `base` = `app_formula:placement/default-weights.json`, the shipped vector. The value sits inside the committed `sweep-bounds.json` range for this axis (`min` 0.0, `max` 16.0), and `simulator/crates/engine/src/policy/params_file.rs::the_sp3_arm_files_are_the_committed_expansion_perturbations` pins the file as the live defaults with only that key moved. **Why 4.0, fixed in the plan's Context before any of this was implemented:** a full-share move of 0.25 costs one point, which is the scale at which the term can reorder candidates the production terms rate closely without overriding them. The moves it actually sees are smaller than that. On a first pick the holding is empty, so every candidate's share goes from 0 to its own, which on a three-hex vertex runs from about 0.33 when the tokens are equal to about 0.45 when one hex carries a 6 or an 8 beside two weak ones, and the *spread between candidates* is therefore a tenth of a share or less, or a few tenths of a point at 4.0. On a second pick the share usually falls, so most candidates take a credit rather than a charge, and the spread is what separates a candidate that doubles down on the pair's best hex from one that spreads it. Only that within-pick spread can move an argmax, because both scorers choose per pick over candidates sharing one holding, so the term's absolute sign on either pick is not a quantity this run measures. One arm rather than two, unlike M-54: the value is pinned by an interpretable calibration rather than guessed from the units, and M-54's own reading is that the smaller weight on a new axis was the stronger one, so a second, larger point is the less informative direction to spend a contrast on.

**Why this A/B is informative, and what a null would mean.** M-56 measured the outcome side within strata where the confound cannot vary. Among pairs touching the same number of producing hexes, the top blockability quartile wins less often than the bottom in every stratum holding real mass: `-9.15pp` at five producing hexes and `-4.67pp` at six, a pair-count-weighted mean of **`-6.65pp`** over the 15,169 of 16,000 pairs those two strata hold, against a preregistered threshold of `-3pp`. Every per-slot row indicates the same way. That is the reading this run tries to convert, and the conversion is not guaranteed. Three things could break it. The surviving gap may be downstream of quantities the formula already prices, since `diversityWeight`, `coverageExponent` and `duplicateNumberPenalty` all reward spreading a pair across resources and tokens, and a pair spread across resources is usually spread across hexes; in that case the term double-counts and should read flat or negative. The gap may be a property of pairs the formula does not choose between, arising from board layouts rather than from picks, in which case there is nothing for a term to steer toward. And the term's per-candidate spread may simply be too small at 4.0 to reorder an argmax often enough to matter. A `better` reading says the formula was blind to standing blockability and paying attention to it wins games. A null at declared power says the surviving gap is carried by quantities already in the formula, which retires the robber-attraction item on evidence after M-48 revisited it and M-56 separated it, and is a real answer rather than an absence of one.

**Why this field.** A formula weight has to be scored against the live formula, so field and reference are both the app-formula spec over `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, per the plan's protocol and following M-51's second invocation, M-52, M-53 and M-54. Every seat runs the post-SP1 composite `heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial` with player trading, the field whose provenance M-51 established and whose play M-56 pinned to the commit range `3765e688..255b5ba1`; the hero seat differs from the other three in its placement weights alone. The field is the same one M-56 read the gap on, which is what makes the two readings comparable: M-56's `-6.65pp` is a property of this field's picks, and this run asks whether a term can improve those picks.

**Design.** `evaluate`, `standard4`, 4 seats, `--domain tuning`, 2000 boards x 2 reps, which is 16,000 paired units over 2000 clusters, `--threads 0`, `--threshold 0.01`, `--alpha 0.05`, reference `base`. That is the plan's screen power for an optional term, matching M-38's placement screens, M-49, M-52, M-53 and M-54.

**Power.** The magnitude this run is powered against is **`-3.59pp`, the smallest per-slot within-stratum weighted mean gap M-56 recorded (slot 0)**, not the `-6.65pp` overall row. The reasoning is M-54's and is taken deliberately: a single unscaled weight has to work at every slot at once, so the conservative magnitude is the weakest slot's, and M-56's per-slot rows run from `-3.59pp` at slot 0 and `-3.69pp` at slot 2 to `-9.19pp` at slot 1 and `-7.49pp` at slot 3. Only a fraction of that is available to a formula term, because the term moves the pick rather than the outcome and moves it only where the concentration ranking disagrees with the ranking the production, diversity and coverage terms already produce. M-38's placement screens at this power resolved effects around 1pp with intervals of roughly `±0.4pp`, and M-54's arms at this power came back with half-widths of `±0.71pp` and `±0.78pp`, so an effect of a few tenths of a point is at the edge of resolution here and anything at or above 1pp is comfortably inside it. The per-hero-seat table from Task 1 is recorded for this arm as it was for M-54's, and it is record-only: it is where a slot pattern would be read if one exists, and it carries no verdict. The pooled verdict is the verdict.

**Command.** Build first, from the repo root, so no compile lands inside a timed window:

```text
RUSTFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo build --release --manifest-path simulator/Cargo.toml -p unsettled-sim
```

Then, from `simulator/`, with `uptime` recorded immediately before and immediately after:

```text
target/release/unsettled-sim evaluate --layout standard4 --seats 4 --domain tuning --field app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --arm base=app_formula:placement/default-weights.json --arm conc=app_formula:placement/arms/sp3_concentration.json --reference base --boards 2000 --reps 2 --policy heuristic-v1-trader-aware-threat-devcards-denial --threads 0 --player-trading --threshold 0.01 --alpha 0.05 --out runs/m57-robber-concentration
```

**Decision rule (fixed before the run).** The plan's standing disposition rule, applied without discretion. `better` records 4.0 as a surviving SP6 candidate. `worse` drops it and the M entry records why the term was expected to help. `equivalent` at this declared power drops it and records the null itself as the answer to the robber-attraction item, closing it on evidence rather than leaving it open. `inconclusive` buys units exactly once, at four times the boards (`--boards 8000`, `--reps` unchanged at 2) in a single retry invocation carrying the same arm, and if the retry is still `inconclusive` the item is recorded unresolved and the run stops there. There is no second value on this axis in either branch: a `worse` or `equivalent` reading does not buy a smaller weight, and a `better` reading does not buy a larger one, because neither is preregistered and adding one after the reading would be choosing the arm from the result. Record only in every branch: no shipped default moves, in `src/engine/weights.ts`, `simulator/placement/default-weights.json`, `simulator/placement/phase-i-candidate-weights.json` or anywhere else, and `sp3_concentration.json` stays the only committed file carrying a nonzero value on this axis. Nothing here reverts the term either: a weight shipped at 0 takes no share and is inert. No entry in `.claude/specs/simulator/gaps.md` moves on this reading in any branch, because the robber-attraction item has never been a gap entry: it is a dropped proposal recorded in `.claude/specs/simulator/placement-programme.md`, revisited there by M-48 and branched there by M-56, and this run appends its outcome to that record.

**Prediction (recorded before the run).** I predict `conc` reads `inconclusive` with a positive point estimate between `+0.3pp` and `+1.0pp`, on 2,500 to 5,000 discordant units of 16,000. Four things drive that. First, the size and consistency of the underlying reading: `-6.65pp` overall with both counted strata negative and all four slots negative is a larger and steadier signal than the port deficit M-53 tried to convert, and a term aimed at it should not land on zero. Second, the reason to expect only a fraction: the diversity, coverage and duplicate-number terms already push the formula away from concentrated pairs, so the term is adding pressure in a direction the formula already leans rather than correcting a blind spot, which is the difference between this axis and the expansion axis M-54 measured. Third, the discordant count is set high on M-54's explicit lesson, that a term with a new input is far more active than its correlation with the existing terms suggests: M-54 predicted 700 to 1,400 and delivered 3,697, so a prediction reasoned the same way should start where that one landed rather than where it started. Fourth, `inconclusive` rather than `better` because the M-54 pattern at this power was an interval straddling the `+1pp` threshold from below, and an effect I expect to be smaller than M-54's should straddle it the same way or sit under it as `equivalent`. Two readings would genuinely change my mind and are named now so they cannot be explained away later. A **negative** point estimate would say the concentration penalty is fighting the production terms on candidates where production was right, which is the double-count failure mode above and would make the surviving M-56 gap a property of pairs rather than of picks. A clean `better` with the interval above `+1pp` would say the existing spread-rewarding terms are much weaker proxies for blockability than they look, and would make the concentration axis, not the expansion axis, the strongest single result of SP3.

**Threshold and alpha.** `±1pp` practical threshold, alpha 0.05. Interval selection is the harness's own rule in `evaluate.rs::try_paired_stats`, the wider of the McNemar and board-clustered intervals.

**Admissibility.** The release binary is built before the measurement starts and the build is confirmed to report `Finished` against a warm fingerprint, which is M-50's lesson. `uptime` is recorded immediately before and immediately after the single `evaluate` invocation and both readings enter the M entry. Zero illegal actions required. The invocation shares its command block with no build, no test suite and no other CPU-heavy job. One run of 32,000 games, two arms against 2000 boards x 2 reps. Throughput should sit close to M-53's rather than M-54's: the term is arithmetic over a hex mask the precompute already carries, with no walk and no allocation on the Rust side, so it is far cheaper per candidate than the expansion walk, and the shipped-weight arm skips it entirely. Timing is a throughput observation only; the outcomes are deterministic in the seeds and unaffected by load. Should the extrapolated wall time exceed five minutes the plan's long-run rule applies, though a formula screen at this power is not expected to approach it. The reference arm's win rate is checked against the symmetric 0.25 corner, as M-51 through M-54 checked theirs. No reproduction check is registered against M-56's numbers, and this is deliberate: M-56's own reproduction clause failed as written and the general rule it left behind is to name the commits a field is held constant against rather than assert a field is unchanged. This run's field is the tree at the run's own commit, whose placement code has moved since M-56 by exactly one term shipping at 0 with a byte-identical corpus, and the reference arm carries the shipped vector, so any drift shows up in the reference's own win rate rather than needing a separate assertion. `simulator/runs/` is gitignored, so the artifact stays out of the tree and the M entry is the record.

**Numbering.** M-56 is the last entry in `.claude/specs/simulator/measurements.md` at this commit, so this run takes M-57. If that file has moved on by the time the run executes, the entry takes the next unused number and this preregistration is cited by filename.
